Sales tax rates vary by state and city. Our guide covers all 50 US states, how to calculate sales tax, and when it applies to your purchases.

What Is Sales Tax and How Does It Work?

Sales tax is a consumption tax imposed by state and local governments on the sale of goods and certain services. When you buy a taxable item, the retailer collects the tax at the point of sale and remits it to the government. Unlike income tax, which is paid annually, sales tax is collected transaction by transaction.

The United States does not have a federal sales tax. Instead, each state sets its own rate, and local governments (counties and cities) can add additional taxes on top of the state rate. This creates a complex patchwork of rates across the country.

How to Calculate Sales Tax

The formula is straightforward:

Sales Tax Amount = Purchase Price x (Tax Rate / 100)

Total Price = Purchase Price + Sales Tax Amount

Example: You buy a laptop for $800 in a state with 7% sales tax.

Sales Tax = $800 x 0.07 = $56

Total Price = $800 + $56 = $856

States With No Sales Tax

Five states have no statewide sales tax:

  • Oregon: No state or local sales tax
  • Montana: No sales tax (some resort areas have local taxes)
  • New Hampshire: No sales tax
  • Delaware: No sales tax
  • Alaska: No state sales tax, but local jurisdictions may charge up to 7.5%

If you live near a border with one of these states, it can be worth crossing the border for large purchases.

States With the Highest Sales Tax Rates

Statewide base rates (before local additions):

  • California: 7.25% (highest state rate in the US)
  • Indiana: 7%
  • Mississippi: 7%
  • Rhode Island: 7%
  • Tennessee: 7%

Combined State and Local Rates

When you add local taxes, the combined rate can be significantly higher than the state rate alone. Some of the highest combined rates in the country:

  • Tennessee: average combined rate around 9.55%
  • Louisiana: average combined rate around 9.55%
  • Arkansas: average combined rate around 9.47%
  • Washington: average combined rate around 9.38%
  • Alabama: average combined rate around 9.25%

Always check the specific rate for your city and county, as rates vary significantly within states.

How to Calculate Total Price With Tax

If you know the tax rate and want to find the total price quickly:

Total Price = Purchase Price x (1 + Tax Rate)

Example: A $200 item with 8.5% tax:

Total = $200 x 1.085 = $217

To find the pre-tax price from a total (reverse calculation):

Pre-tax Price = Total Price / (1 + Tax Rate)

Example: You paid $108.50 total with 8.5% tax:

Pre-tax = $108.50 / 1.085 = $100

Online Shopping and Sales Tax: The Wayfair Ruling

Before 2018, online retailers only had to collect sales tax in states where they had a physical presence. The Supreme Court's 2018 South Dakota v. Wayfair decision changed everything. Now, states can require online retailers to collect sales tax even without a physical presence, based on economic nexus thresholds (typically $100,000 in sales or 200 transactions per year).

This means most major online retailers now collect sales tax in most states. However, smaller online sellers may still not collect tax in all states, which technically means the buyer owes use tax — though enforcement is rare for individuals.

Tips for Tax-Conscious Shopping

  • Time large purchases strategically: Many states hold sales tax holidays (typically in August for back-to-school shopping) where certain items are exempt from sales tax.
  • Know your exemptions: Many states exempt groceries, prescription medications, and clothing from sales tax.
  • Compare total prices: When comparing prices across retailers, always factor in the final price including tax.
  • Keep receipts for business purchases: If you use items for business, sales tax paid may be deductible.
  • Check for tax-exempt status: Nonprofits, resellers, and certain businesses may qualify for sales tax exemptions with proper documentation.
