The metric vs imperial debate has confused people for decades. Here's a simple guide to the most common unit conversions you'll actually need.

A Brief History of Metric vs Imperial

The world uses two main measurement systems: the metric system (officially called the International System of Units, or SI) and the imperial system. The metric system was developed in France in the late 18th century and has been adopted by virtually every country in the world — except the United States, Liberia, and Myanmar, which still primarily use imperial measurements.

The metric system is based on powers of 10, making conversions simple. The imperial system evolved from a hodgepodge of historical units, many based on human body parts (the foot, the inch from the thumb's width), making conversions more complex.

The Most Important Conversion Factors

Length Conversions

  • 1 inch = 2.54 centimeters (exact)
  • 1 foot = 30.48 centimeters
  • 1 yard = 0.9144 meters
  • 1 mile = 1.60934 kilometers
  • 1 kilometer = 0.62137 miles
  • 1 meter = 3.28084 feet

Weight Conversions

  • 1 pound = 0.453592 kilograms
  • 1 kilogram = 2.20462 pounds
  • 1 ounce = 28.3495 grams
  • 1 stone (UK) = 6.35029 kilograms = 14 pounds

Temperature Conversions

  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: C = (F - 32) x 5/9
  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: F = C x 9/5 + 32
  • Key reference points: 0C = 32F (freezing), 100C = 212F (boiling), 37C = 98.6F (body temperature), 20C = 68F (room temperature)

Volume Conversions

  • 1 US gallon = 3.78541 liters
  • 1 liter = 0.264172 US gallons
  • 1 fluid ounce = 29.5735 milliliters
  • 1 cup = 236.588 milliliters
  • 1 pint = 473.176 milliliters
  • 1 quart = 946.353 milliliters

Common Conversion Mistakes

  • Confusing US and UK measurements: A US gallon (3.785 L) is different from a UK imperial gallon (4.546 L). A US pint is 16 fl oz; a UK pint is 20 fl oz.
  • Mixing up mass and weight: Kilograms measure mass; pounds technically measure force (weight). In everyday use they are treated as equivalent, but in scientific contexts this matters.
  • Forgetting to convert units consistently: If you convert distance to km but leave speed in mph, your calculations will be wrong.

Why Does the US Still Use Imperial?

The US has attempted to convert to metric multiple times. The Metric Conversion Act of 1975 made metric the preferred system for trade and commerce, but conversion was voluntary. The cost and disruption of changing road signs, manufacturing equipment, and consumer habits has made full conversion politically difficult. The US does use metric extensively in science, medicine, military, and international trade.

Practical Conversion Tips for Travelers

When traveling between metric and imperial countries:

  • Speed: Multiply km/h by 0.62 to get mph. A 100 km/h speed limit is about 62 mph.
  • Temperature: A quick estimate: double the Celsius temperature and add 30 to get approximate Fahrenheit. (20C x 2 + 30 = 70F, actual is 68F — close enough for weather.)
  • Distance: 1 kilometer is roughly 0.6 miles. 5 km is about 3 miles.
  • Weight: Divide kilograms by 2.2 to get pounds. 70 kg is about 154 lbs.

Cooking Conversions

Recipes from different countries use different measurement systems:

  • 1 tablespoon = 15 ml
  • 1 teaspoon = 5 ml
  • 1 cup = 240 ml (US)
  • 1 stick of butter = 113 grams = 1/2 cup
  • 350F oven = 175C (common baking temperature)
  • 400F oven = 200C

Scientific Use of the Metric System

All scientific work worldwide uses the metric system. The SI base units are: meter (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), ampere (electric current), kelvin (temperature), mole (amount of substance), and candela (luminous intensity). Using a consistent system eliminates conversion errors — a lesson learned painfully when NASA lost the Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999 due to a metric/imperial unit mismatch.
