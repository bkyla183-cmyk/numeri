Scientific Calculator Tips: 10 Functions You're Probably Not Using

Most people use only 20% of their scientific calculator's capabilities. Here are 10 powerful functions that can save you time and effort.

Why Most People Use Only 10% of Their Calculator

A scientific calculator — whether physical or digital — contains dozens of functions beyond basic arithmetic. Most students and professionals use only the four basic operations plus maybe square root and percentage. But the advanced functions can save enormous time on complex calculations in math, science, finance, and engineering. Here are 10 powerful functions you should start using today.

1. Memory Functions (M+, M−, MR, MC)

Memory functions let you store intermediate results without writing them down.

  • M+: Add current display to memory
  • M−: Subtract current display from memory
  • MR (Memory Recall): Display stored value
  • MC (Memory Clear): Clear memory

Example: Calculate (15 × 8) + (23 × 4) without writing anything down:

  1. Calculate 15 × 8 = 120, press M+
  2. Calculate 23 × 4 = 92, press M+
  3. Press MR → displays 212

2. Scientific Notation (EXP or EE)

For very large or very small numbers, scientific notation is essential. The EXP or EE button enters the exponent.

Example: Enter 6.022 × 10²³ (Avogadro's number):

  • Press: 6.022 EXP 23
  • Display shows: 6.022E23

Example: Calculate the mass of an electron (9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg):

  • Press: 9.109 EXP −31

3. Trigonometric Functions (sin, cos, tan)

Essential for geometry, physics, and engineering. Always check whether your calculator is in degrees or radians mode (DEG vs RAD).

Common values to know:

  • sin(30°) = 0.5
  • cos(60°) = 0.5
  • tan(45°) = 1
  • sin(90°) = 1

Example: Find the height of a building if you're 50m away and the angle of elevation is 35°:

  • Height = 50 × tan(35°) = 50 × 0.7002 = 35.0 meters

4. Inverse Trigonometric Functions (sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹)

These find the angle when you know the ratio. Usually accessed via SHIFT or 2nd + the trig function.

Example: A ramp rises 3 meters over a horizontal distance of 12 meters. What is the angle?

  • Angle = tan⁻¹(3/12) = tan⁻¹(0.25) = 14.04°

5. Logarithms (log and ln)

  • log: Base-10 logarithm (common log)
  • ln: Natural logarithm (base e)

Applications:

  • pH calculation: pH = −log[H⁺]
  • Decibel calculation: dB = 10 × log(P₂/P₁)
  • Compound interest: n = log(FV/PV) ÷ log(1+r)
  • Half-life: t = ln(2) ÷ λ

Example: How many years to double money at 7% annual return?

  • n = log(2) ÷ log(1.07) = 0.3010 ÷ 0.02938 = 10.24 years

(This is the Rule of 72: 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10.3 years — a useful mental shortcut)

6. Powers and Roots (x^y, x^(1/y))

The x^y button raises x to any power. For roots, use x^(1/n).

Examples:

  • 2^10 = 1,024 (press: 2, x^y, 10)
  • Cube root of 125: 125^(1/3) = 5 (press: 125, x^y, (1÷3))
  • 5th root of 32: 32^(1/5) = 2

7. Factorial (n!)

Factorial calculates n × (n−1) × (n−2) × ... × 1. Essential for combinations and permutations.

Examples:

  • 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120
  • 10! = 3,628,800

Application: How many ways can 8 people sit in 8 chairs? 8! = 40,320 arrangements.

8. Combinations and Permutations (nCr, nPr)

  • nCr (combinations): Number of ways to choose r items from n, where order doesn't matter
  • nPr (permutations): Number of ways to choose r items from n, where order matters

Example: How many 5-card poker hands are possible from a 52-card deck?

  • 52C5 = 52! ÷ (5! × 47!) = 2,598,960 hands

Example: How many 3-digit PIN codes from digits 0–9 (no repeats)?

  • 10P3 = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720 PINs

9. Hyperbolic Functions (sinh, cosh, tanh)

Used in engineering, physics, and advanced mathematics. Hyperbolic functions describe the shape of hanging cables, special relativity, and heat transfer.

Example: The catenary curve (shape of a hanging chain) uses cosh:

  • y = a × cosh(x/a)

Less commonly needed in everyday calculations, but essential for engineering students.

10. Degree-Minute-Second Conversion (DMS)

Converts between decimal degrees and degrees-minutes-seconds format, used in navigation and surveying.

Example: Convert 35.75° to DMS:

  • Degrees: 35°
  • Minutes: 0.75 × 60 = 45'
  • Result: 35° 45' 0"

Example: Convert 40° 30' 18" to decimal:

  • 40 + (30/60) + (18/3600) = 40.505°

Bonus: Using the ANS Key

The ANS (Answer) key recalls the last calculated result. This is invaluable for chained calculations:

Example: Calculate √(3² + 4²):

  1. 3 x² = 9
  2. + 4 x² = 25 (calculator shows 9 + 16 = 25)
  3. √ANS = 5
