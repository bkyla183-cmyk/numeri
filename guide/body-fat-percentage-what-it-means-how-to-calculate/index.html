Body Fat Percentage: What It Means and How to Calculate It

Body fat percentage is a better health indicator than BMI alone. Learn what healthy ranges look like and how to calculate your body fat percentage.

Body Fat Percentage vs BMI

While BMI (Body Mass Index) is a widely used health metric, body fat percentage gives a more accurate picture of your body composition. BMI only considers your height and weight, while body fat percentage tells you exactly how much of your body is fat versus lean mass (muscle, bone, organs, and water).

Two people can have the same BMI but very different body fat percentages. A muscular athlete and a sedentary person of the same height and weight will have the same BMI, but the athlete may have 12% body fat while the sedentary person has 28%.

Healthy Body Fat Ranges by Gender and Age

For Men:

  • Essential fat: 2-5% (minimum needed for basic physiological function)
  • Athletic: 6-13%
  • Fitness: 14-17%
  • Average: 18-24%
  • Obese: 25% and above

For Women:

  • Essential fat: 10-13% (women need more essential fat due to hormonal and reproductive functions)
  • Athletic: 14-20%
  • Fitness: 21-24%
  • Average: 25-31%
  • Obese: 32% and above

As you age, healthy body fat ranges shift slightly upward. A 50-year-old man at 22% body fat is in a different health context than a 25-year-old at the same percentage.

Methods to Measure Body Fat

DEXA Scan (Dual-Energy X-ray Absorptiometry)

The gold standard for body composition measurement. A DEXA scan uses low-dose X-rays to differentiate between bone, fat, and lean tissue. It is highly accurate but requires a medical facility and costs between $50 and $150 per scan.

Hydrostatic Weighing

Also called underwater weighing, this method measures body density by comparing your weight on land to your weight submerged in water. Very accurate but inconvenient and not widely available.

Skinfold Calipers

A trained technician uses calipers to measure the thickness of fat folds at multiple sites on the body (typically 3, 7, or 9 sites). Accuracy depends heavily on the skill of the measurer. Inexpensive and widely available.

Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA)

Sends a small electrical current through the body. Fat conducts electricity differently than muscle. Found in consumer scales and handheld devices. Convenient but accuracy varies with hydration levels.

The Navy Method

A simple formula using body measurements that you can do at home with a tape measure.

The Navy Method Formula

For men:

Body Fat % = 86.010 x log10(waist - neck) - 70.041 x log10(height) + 36.76

For women:

Body Fat % = 163.205 x log10(waist + hip - neck) - 97.684 x log10(height) - 78.387

All measurements in centimeters. Measure waist at the narrowest point, hips at the widest point, and neck just below the larynx.

How Body Fat Affects Health

Excess body fat, particularly visceral fat (fat stored around internal organs), is associated with:

  • Increased risk of type 2 diabetes
  • Cardiovascular disease and high blood pressure
  • Metabolic syndrome
  • Certain cancers
  • Sleep apnea
  • Joint problems

Visceral Fat vs Subcutaneous Fat

  • Subcutaneous fat: Fat stored just under the skin. Visible as the fat you can pinch. Less metabolically dangerous.
  • Visceral fat: Fat stored deep in the abdominal cavity around organs. Cannot be seen or pinched. Highly metabolically active and strongly linked to disease risk.

A large waist circumference (over 40 inches for men, 35 inches for women) is a warning sign of excess visceral fat even if your overall body fat percentage seems acceptable.

Practical Tips for Reducing Body Fat Percentage

  • Create a moderate calorie deficit (300-500 calories per day) to lose fat without losing muscle
  • Prioritize protein intake (0.7-1 gram per pound of body weight) to preserve lean mass
  • Incorporate resistance training to maintain and build muscle while losing fat
  • Include cardiovascular exercise for overall health and additional calorie burn
  • Get 7-9 hours of sleep — poor sleep increases cortisol, which promotes fat storage
  • Reduce processed foods, added sugars, and alcohol
  • Be patient — sustainable fat loss is 0.5-1% body fat per month
