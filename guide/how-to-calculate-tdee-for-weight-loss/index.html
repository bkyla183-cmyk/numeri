How to Calculate Your TDEE for Weight Loss

Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is the key to successful weight loss. Learn how to calculate it and use it to reach your fitness goals.

What Is TDEE?

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is the total number of calories your body burns in a single day. It accounts for everything: your resting metabolism, the energy used to digest food, and all physical activity. Knowing your TDEE is the foundation of any successful weight loss, maintenance, or muscle-building plan.

Unlike fad diets that give you a generic 1,200-calorie target, TDEE gives you a personalized calorie number based on your actual body and lifestyle.

The Two Components of TDEE

TDEE is made up of two main parts:

  • Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): The calories your body burns at complete rest just to maintain basic functions — breathing, circulation, cell repair, and temperature regulation. BMR accounts for roughly 60-75% of your total calorie burn.
  • Activity Factor: A multiplier applied to your BMR to account for your daily movement and exercise.

Calculating BMR: The Mifflin-St Jeor Formula

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula is considered the most accurate for most people:

For men:

BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) + 5

For women:

BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age) - 161

Activity Multipliers

Once you have your BMR, multiply it by the appropriate activity factor:

  • Sedentary (1.2): Little or no exercise, desk job
  • Lightly active (1.375): Light exercise 1-3 days per week
  • Moderately active (1.55): Moderate exercise 3-5 days per week
  • Very active (1.725): Hard exercise 6-7 days per week
  • Extra active (1.9): Very hard exercise, physical job, or twice-daily training

Worked Example

Let us calculate TDEE for a 30-year-old male, 70 kg, 175 cm tall, who exercises moderately 3-5 days per week:

Step 1 — Calculate BMR:

BMR = (10 × 70) + (6.25 × 175) - (5 × 30) + 5

BMR = 700 + 1,093.75 - 150 + 5 = 1,648.75 calories

Step 2 — Apply activity multiplier:

TDEE = 1,648.75 × 1.55 = 2,555 calories per day

This person needs approximately 2,555 calories per day to maintain their current weight.

Creating a Calorie Deficit for Weight Loss

To lose weight, you need to consume fewer calories than your TDEE. A deficit of 500 calories per day leads to approximately 1 pound (0.45 kg) of fat loss per week, because one pound of fat contains roughly 3,500 calories.

  • Moderate deficit (500 cal/day): ~1 lb/week loss — sustainable and recommended for most people
  • Aggressive deficit (750-1,000 cal/day): ~1.5-2 lbs/week — acceptable for those with significant weight to lose
  • Very low calorie (below 1,200 for women, 1,500 for men): Not recommended — leads to muscle loss, nutrient deficiencies, and metabolic slowdown

For our example above, a weight loss target of 2,055 calories per day (2,555 - 500) would produce steady, sustainable fat loss.

Common Mistakes in TDEE Calculation

  • Overestimating activity level: Most people choose "moderately active" when they are actually sedentary or lightly active. Be honest about your real daily movement.
  • Not accounting for non-exercise activity (NEAT): Fidgeting, walking to your car, and household chores all count. Some people burn 500+ more calories per day than others just through incidental movement.
  • Using the wrong formula: The Harris-Benedict formula (older) tends to overestimate BMR. Mifflin-St Jeor is more accurate for modern populations.
  • Ignoring body composition: TDEE formulas are based on total weight, not lean mass. Very muscular people will have a higher actual BMR than the formula predicts.

Adjusting TDEE as You Lose Weight

As you lose weight, your BMR decreases because you have less body mass to maintain. This is why weight loss often plateaus after several weeks. Recalculate your TDEE every 10-15 pounds of weight loss and adjust your calorie target accordingly. This adaptive approach keeps your deficit consistent and your progress steady throughout your weight loss journey.
