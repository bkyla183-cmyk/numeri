The Basic Percentage Formula

Percentages are one of the most practical math concepts in everyday life. Whether you are calculating a discount at the store, figuring out how much to tip at a restaurant, or understanding your grade on a test, percentages are everywhere. The word percent literally means per hundred, so 45% means 45 out of every 100.

The basic formula is: Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100. You can run any of these formulas instantly with our Percentage Calculator.

5 Common Percentage Calculations

1. What Percentage Is X of Y?

Formula: (X / Y) × 100

Example: What percentage is 30 of 120?

(30 / 120) × 100 = 25%

Use this when you want to express one number as a portion of another — like what percentage of your budget goes to rent.

2. What Is X% of Y?

Formula: (X / 100) × Y

Example: What is 15% of $80?

(15 / 100) × $80 = $12

Use this for calculating tips, discounts, or tax amounts.

3. Percentage Increase

Formula: ((New Value - Old Value) / Old Value) × 100

Example: A salary increased from $50,000 to $55,000.

(($55,000 - $50,000) / $50,000) × 100 = 10% increase

4. Percentage Decrease

Formula: ((Old Value - New Value) / Old Value) × 100

Example: A product dropped from $200 to $150.

(($200 - $150) / $200) × 100 = 25% decrease

5. Percentage Difference

Formula: (|Value1 - Value2| / ((Value1 + Value2) / 2)) × 100

Use this when comparing two values without a clear baseline — like comparing prices at two different stores.

Real-World Examples

Shopping Discounts

A jacket is 30% off its $120 original price.

Discount amount: (30 / 100) × $120 = $36

Sale price: $120 - $36 = $84 (also checkable with our Discount Calculator).

Sales Tax

You buy a $250 laptop in a state with 8% sales tax.

Tax amount: (8 / 100) × $250 = $20

Total price: $250 + $20 = $270

Restaurant Tips

Your bill is $65 and you want to leave an 18% tip.

Tip amount: (18 / 100) × $65 = $11.70

Total: $65 + $11.70 = $76.70

Grade Percentages

You scored 43 out of 50 on a quiz.

Grade: (43 / 50) × 100 = 86%

Interest Rates

A savings account pays 4.5% APY on $5,000.

Annual interest: (4.5 / 100) × $5,000 = $225

Mental Math Tricks for Quick Percentage Estimates

  • 10% trick: Move the decimal point one place left. 10% of $240 = $24.
  • 5% trick: Find 10% then halve it. 5% of $240 = $12.
  • 15% tip: Find 10% + 5%. 10% of $80 = $8, plus 5% = $4, total tip = $12.
  • 20% trick: Find 10% and double it. 20% of $85 = $17.
  • 1% trick: Move the decimal two places left. 1% of $1,500 = $15.
  • 25% trick: Divide by 4. 25% of $400 = $100.
  • 50% trick: Simply divide by 2. 50% of $180 = $90.

Common Percentage Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing percentage points with percentages: If interest rates rise from 2% to 3%, that is a 1 percentage point increase but a 50% increase in the rate itself.
  • Reversing the formula: Because multiplication is commutative, 20% of 50 equals 50% of 20 (both equal 10), which is a useful mental-math shortcut.
  • Adding percentages incorrectly: A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does NOT return to the original value. You end up with 96% of the original.
  • Forgetting the base: A 50% discount on a $100 item saves $50. A 50% discount on a $200 item saves $100. Always identify the base before calculating.

Mastering percentage calculations takes practice, but once you internalize these formulas and tricks, you will find yourself using them confidently every single day.
