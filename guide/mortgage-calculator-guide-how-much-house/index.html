Understanding the Mortgage Payment Formula

Buying a home is the largest financial decision most people will ever make. Before you fall in love with a house, you need to understand exactly how much it will cost you each month — and over the life of the loan. The mortgage payment formula gives you that answer.

The standard formula for a fixed-rate mortgage payment is:

M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]

Where:

  • M = monthly payment
  • P = principal loan amount
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12)
  • n = total number of payments (loan term in years times 12)

Worked Example: $300,000 at 6.5% for 30 Years

Let us calculate the monthly payment on a $300,000 mortgage at 6.5% interest for 30 years:

  • P = $300,000
  • Annual rate = 6.5%, so monthly rate r = 0.065 / 12 = 0.005417
  • n = 30 x 12 = 360 payments

M = 300,000 x [0.005417 x (1.005417)^360] / [(1.005417)^360 - 1]

M = approximately $1,896 per month

Over 30 years, you will pay a total of $682,560 — meaning $382,560 goes to interest alone on a $300,000 loan.

The 28/36 Rule for Affordability

Lenders use the 28/36 rule to determine how much house you can afford:

  • 28% rule: Your monthly mortgage payment (principal + interest + taxes + insurance) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income.
  • 36% rule: Your total monthly debt payments (mortgage + car loans + student loans + credit cards) should not exceed 36% of your gross monthly income.

Example: If your gross monthly income is $7,000:

  • Maximum mortgage payment: $7,000 x 0.28 = $1,960
  • Maximum total debt: $7,000 x 0.36 = $2,520

What Is Included in a Mortgage Payment (PITI)?

Your actual monthly payment is typically more than just principal and interest:

  • P — Principal: The portion that reduces your loan balance
  • I — Interest: The cost of borrowing money
  • T — Taxes: Property taxes, usually collected monthly and held in escrow
  • I — Insurance: Homeowners insurance, also typically escrowed
  • PMI: Private mortgage insurance, required if your down payment is less than 20%

Down Payment Impact

Your down payment directly affects your loan amount, monthly payment, and whether you need PMI:

  • 3-5% down: Minimum for conventional loans; PMI required
  • 10% down: Lower PMI costs
  • 20% down: No PMI required; saves $100-200/month on a typical loan
  • 20%+ down: Best rates and lowest monthly payment

On a $300,000 home, a 20% down payment ($60,000) means you only borrow $240,000, reducing your monthly payment to approximately $1,517.

How Interest Rate Affects Total Cost

Even a small change in interest rate has a massive impact over 30 years:

  • 5.5% rate: $1,703/month, total interest = $313,080
  • 6.5% rate: $1,896/month, total interest = $382,560
  • 7.5% rate: $2,098/month, total interest = $455,280

A 1% rate difference on a $300,000 loan costs nearly $70,000 more over 30 years.

15-Year vs 30-Year Mortgage

  • 30-year mortgage: Lower monthly payment, more total interest paid, more cash flow flexibility
  • 15-year mortgage: Higher monthly payment (roughly 40% more), dramatically less interest paid, builds equity faster

On a $300,000 loan at 6.5%: the 30-year costs $382,560 in interest; the 15-year costs only about $155,000 in interest — a savings of over $227,000.

Tips for Getting a Lower Rate

  • Improve your credit score (aim for 740+)
  • Save a larger down payment
  • Shop at least 3-5 lenders and compare APR, not just rate
  • Consider buying mortgage points to lower your rate
  • Get pre-approved before house hunting
  • Lock your rate once you find a good one

The Pre-Approval Process

Pre-approval is a lender's conditional commitment to lend you a specific amount. It requires submitting income documents, tax returns, bank statements, and authorizing a credit check. Pre-approval strengthens your offer in competitive markets and helps you shop within your actual budget — not just what you hope to afford. Always get pre-approved before making an offer on a home.
