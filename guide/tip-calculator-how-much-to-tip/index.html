The American Tipping Culture: What You Need to Know

Tipping in the United States is not optional — it's a social contract. Unlike most countries where service workers earn a living wage, many US service workers depend on tips for the majority of their income. Federal law allows employers to pay tipped workers as little as $2.13/hour (the federal tipped minimum wage), with tips expected to bring total compensation to at least $7.25/hour. In practice, tips often represent 50–80% of a server's income.

Understanding when, how much, and how to calculate tips helps you navigate American service culture confidently.

Standard Tipping Percentages by Service Type

Restaurants (sit-down):

  • Poor service: 10–12%
  • Adequate service: 15%
  • Good service: 18–20%
  • Excellent service: 22–25%+

Bars and bartenders: $1–2 per drink, or 15–20% of tab

Food delivery: 15–20% of order total (minimum $3–5 for small orders)

Coffee shops/counter service: $1–2 per order, or 10–15% (optional but appreciated)

Hotel housekeeping: $2–5 per night, left daily (different staff may clean each day)

Hotel bellhop/porter: $1–2 per bag

Hotel concierge: $5–20 for special arrangements

Taxi/rideshare (Uber, Lyft): 15–20%

Hair salon/barber: 15–20% of service cost

Spa services (massage, facial): 15–20%

Movers: $20–50 per mover for a full-day move

Valet parking: $2–5 when retrieving your car

Tour guides: $5–10 per person for group tours; 15–20% for private tours

How to Calculate a Tip: Three Methods

Method 1: The Percentage Method

Tip = Bill × (Tip% ÷ 100)

For a $67.50 bill at 20%:

$67.50 × 0.20 = $13.50 tip

Total: $67.50 + $13.50 = $81.00

Method 2: The Double-and-Move Method (for 20%)

  1. Move the decimal one place left (10%): $67.50 → $6.75
  2. Double it (20%): $6.75 × 2 = $13.50

Method 3: The Tax Doubling Method

In many US states, sales tax is 7–10%. Doubling the tax gives an approximate 14–20% tip.

For a $67.50 bill with 8% tax ($5.40):

Double the tax: $5.40 × 2 = $10.80 (approximately 16% tip)

Should You Tip on Pre-Tax or Post-Tax Amount?

Technically, tipping on the pre-tax amount is correct — you're tipping for service, not for the government's cut. However, the difference is small:

$80 bill with 8% tax = $86.40 total

  • 20% tip on pre-tax: $80 × 0.20 = $16.00
  • 20% tip on post-tax: $86.40 × 0.20 = $17.28

Difference: $1.28. Most people tip on the total for simplicity, and servers appreciate the slightly higher amount.

Splitting the Bill: Fair Division

Equal split: Total (including tip) ÷ number of people

$120 bill + 20% tip = $144 total ÷ 4 people = $36 each

Itemized split: Each person pays for their items plus a proportional share of the tip.

Person A ordered $35 worth of food. Total food bill was $120. Their share of a $24 tip:

($35 ÷ $120) × $24 = $7.00 tip share

Person A pays: $35 + $7 = $42

Tip apps: Splitwise, Venmo, and similar apps handle complex splits automatically.

When Gratuity Is Already Included

Many restaurants automatically add an 18–20% gratuity for:

  • Parties of 6 or more
  • Banquet or private dining events
  • Some tourist-area restaurants

Always check your bill for "gratuity included," "service charge," or "auto-gratuity" before adding an additional tip. Adding 20% on top of an already-included 18% gratuity is a common mistake.

Tipping in the Digital Age: The Tip Screen Problem

Tablet payment systems (Square, Toast, Clover) now present tip screens at counter-service establishments that previously didn't expect tips — coffee shops, fast food, ice cream shops. These screens often suggest 18%, 20%, and 25% as options, with "No Tip" as a small, awkward button.

You are not obligated to tip at counter-service establishments. Tipping is most appropriate when someone provides table service, delivers food to your home, or performs a personal service. For counter service where you order and pick up your own food, tipping is optional and typically 10–15% if you choose to.

International Tipping Norms

If you're traveling abroad, know that tipping customs vary dramatically:

  • Japan: Tipping is considered rude — service workers may refuse tips
  • Australia/New Zealand: Tipping is not expected; 10% for exceptional service
  • UK/Europe: 10–15% for sit-down restaurants; not expected at bars
  • Canada: Similar to US — 15–20% expected at restaurants
  • Mexico: 10–15% at restaurants; $1–2/day for hotel housekeeping
