Ideal Weight Calculator: What Should You Actually Weigh?

Ideal weight isn't one-size-fits-all. Learn how different formulas calculate healthy weight and what actually matters for your health.

Why There Is No Single Ideal Weight

The concept of an "ideal weight" is more complex than a single number on a scale. Your ideal weight depends on your height, frame size, age, sex, and body composition. Two people of the same height can have very different healthy weights based on their muscle mass, bone density, and body frame.

Rather than chasing a specific number, focus on a healthy weight range and the health metrics that actually matter: blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, and body fat percentage.

The Main Ideal Weight Formulas

Several formulas have been developed over the decades to estimate ideal body weight. Each was originally designed for clinical use (calculating medication dosages) rather than as a fitness goal.

Devine Formula (1974)

The most widely used formula in clinical settings:

  • Men: Ideal Weight (kg) = 50 + 2.3 x (height in inches - 60)
  • Women: Ideal Weight (kg) = 45.5 + 2.3 x (height in inches - 60)

Example for a 5'10" (70 inch) man:

Ideal Weight = 50 + 2.3 x (70 - 60) = 50 + 23 = 73 kg (161 lbs)

Example for a 5'6" (66 inch) woman:

Ideal Weight = 45.5 + 2.3 x (66 - 60) = 45.5 + 13.8 = 59.3 kg (131 lbs)

Robinson Formula (1983)

  • Men: 52 kg + 1.9 kg per inch over 5 feet
  • Women: 49 kg + 1.7 kg per inch over 5 feet

Miller Formula (1983)

  • Men: 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg per inch over 5 feet
  • Women: 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg per inch over 5 feet

Hamwi Formula (1964)

  • Men: 106 lbs for first 5 feet + 6 lbs per inch over 5 feet
  • Women: 100 lbs for first 5 feet + 5 lbs per inch over 5 feet

BMI-Based Healthy Weight Range

The BMI-based approach gives a range rather than a single number. A healthy BMI is 18.5-24.9.

Healthy Weight Range = BMI range x height (m)^2

For a 5'10" (1.778 m) person:

  • Lower bound: 18.5 x (1.778)^2 = 18.5 x 3.16 = 58.5 kg (129 lbs)
  • Upper bound: 24.9 x (1.778)^2 = 24.9 x 3.16 = 78.7 kg (173 lbs)

This gives a 44-pound range, which is more realistic than a single number.

The Role of Frame Size

Body frame size (small, medium, large) affects how much you should weigh at a given height. A large-framed person naturally has heavier bones and a larger skeletal structure.

To estimate frame size, measure your wrist circumference:

  • Women under 5'2": Small = under 5.5", Medium = 5.5-5.75", Large = over 5.75"
  • Women 5'2" to 5'5": Small = under 6", Medium = 6-6.25", Large = over 6.25"
  • Women over 5'5": Small = under 6.25", Medium = 6.25-6.5", Large = over 6.5"
  • Men: Small = under 6.5", Medium = 6.5-7.5", Large = over 7.5"

Large-framed individuals can be at the higher end of the healthy weight range; small-framed individuals at the lower end.

Muscle Mass and Body Composition

All ideal weight formulas have a critical limitation: they do not account for body composition. A 200-pound bodybuilder with 8% body fat is far healthier than a 160-pound sedentary person with 30% body fat, even though the formulas might suggest the bodybuilder is "overweight."

This is why athletes and muscular individuals often appear "overweight" by formula standards. The formulas were designed for average body compositions and are not appropriate for highly muscular individuals.

Why Athletes Weigh More Than "Ideal"

Muscle is denser than fat — a cubic inch of muscle weighs more than a cubic inch of fat. Elite athletes often weigh significantly more than ideal weight formulas suggest because they carry substantially more muscle mass. This extra weight is healthy and beneficial, not a problem to be corrected.

Healthy Weight Ranges for Common Heights

These ranges are based on a healthy BMI of 18.5-24.9:

  • 5'0" (152 cm): 95-128 lbs (43-58 kg)
  • 5'4" (163 cm): 108-145 lbs (49-66 kg)
  • 5'6" (168 cm): 115-154 lbs (52-70 kg)
  • 5'8" (173 cm): 122-164 lbs (55-74 kg)
  • 5'10" (178 cm): 129-174 lbs (59-79 kg)
  • 6'0" (183 cm): 136-184 lbs (62-83 kg)
  • 6'2" (188 cm): 144-194 lbs (65-88 kg)

The Importance of Focusing on Health Metrics Over a Number

Rather than fixating on a specific weight, focus on measurable health indicators:

  • Blood pressure (target: below 120/80)
  • Fasting blood glucose (target: below 100 mg/dL)
  • Total cholesterol and LDL levels
  • Waist circumference (target: under 40" for men, 35" for women)
  • Body fat percentage (see healthy ranges by gender)
  • Energy levels, sleep quality, and physical fitness

These metrics tell you far more about your actual health than any number on a scale.
