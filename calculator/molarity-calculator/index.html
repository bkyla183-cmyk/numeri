How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Molarity Calculator computes molar solution concentration M (mol/L) and millimolarity (mM) from solute moles and total solution volume in liters.

Molarity expresses chemical concentration per liter of finished solution rather than per liter of added solvent—making reaction stoichiometry, titrations, and buffer recipes exact across analytical and biochemistry laboratories.

Governing Equation
M = n / V | mM = M × 1,000

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Dissolving 0.50 moles of NaCl in water and diluting to a final flask volume of 2.00 liters.

  • Divide moles by liters of finished solution: 0.5 mol / 2.0 L = 0.25 mol/L (0.25 M).
  • Convert to millimolar: 0.25 × 1,000 = 250.00 mM.

Result: 0.25 mol / L (250.00 mM)

Every 100 mL aliquot drawn from this volumetric flask contains 0.025 moles of solute.

Adding Solute to 2.0 L of Water Instead of Making Up to 2.0 L Total

Adding solid solute onto 2.0 L of solvent yields >2.0 L total volume, lowering final concentration below 0.25 M.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Molarity (M) and Molality (m)?

Molarity equals moles of solute per liter of total solution (mol/L) and varies slightly with temperature as liquid volume expands. Molality equals moles of solute per kilogram of solvent and stays temperature-independent.

How do you prepare a 1 M solution properly in a lab?

Dissolve 1 mole of solute in slightly less than 1 L of solvent inside a volumetric flask, then dilute carefully up to the 1.000 L calibration mark—do not simply add 1 L of water on top of solid solute.

How can I convert molarity back to grams of solute?

Multiply Molarity (mol/L) × Volume (L) to obtain total moles, then multiply by the compound’s molar mass (g/mol).
