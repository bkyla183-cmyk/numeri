How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) Calculator applies Erik Widmark’s pharmacokinetic distribution equation across US Standard Drinks (14g ethanol), Australia/NZ Standard Drinks (10g), and UK Alcohol Units (8g) to model theoretical peak BAC, current BAC after hepatic metabolism, and estimated time to 0.00%.

Alcohol distributes primarily across total body water rather than adipose tissue—meaning body weight and biological sex (Widmark volume-of-distribution constant r = 0.68 male, 0.55 female) dramatically alter peak blood concentration per drink. Meanwhile, the human liver oxidizes ethanol via zero-order kinetics at roughly 0.015% per hour regardless of coffee, cold showers, or exercise.

Governing Equation
BAC% = max(0, [Alcohol_g ÷ (Weight_g × r)] × 100 − β × Hours)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A 175 lb (79.4 kg) male consumes 3 US Standard Drinks (14g each = 42g ethanol) over 2.0 hours since the first drink.

  • Gross unmetabolized peak: [42 ÷ (79,378.7 × 0.68)] × 100 = 42 ÷ 53,977.5 × 100 = 0.0778% (~0.078%).
  • Subtract 2.0 hours of zero-order hepatic clearance (0.015%/hr × 2.0 = 0.030%): 0.0778% − 0.030% = 0.048%.
  • Compute remaining sobriety clearance time: 0.048% ÷ 0.015%/hr = 3.2 hours to reach 0.00%.

Result: 0.048% Estimated Current BAC (48 mg/100mL · ~3.2 hrs to 0.00%)

While below 0.08%, judgment and reaction speed remain impaired, and 0.048% exceeds 0.02% commercial/zero-tolerance limits and approaches the 0.05% AU/NZ/Scotland limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do US, Australia/NZ, and UK standard drinks give different BAC estimates?

A US Standard Drink contains 14 grams of pure ethanol, an Australia/NZ Standard Drink contains 10 grams, and a UK Alcohol Unit contains 8 grams. Selecting your exact regional standard ensures 3 drinks aren’t miscounted by 40%–75%.

Why does the Widmark factor differ between biological males (0.68) and females (0.55)?

Ethanol dissolves in total body water rather than body fat. Because average female physiology carries a slightly lower total-body-water fraction per kilogram, an identical dose of pure alcohol produces a higher blood concentration.

Can coffee, water, or exercise speed up alcohol elimination below 0.015%/hr?

No. Caffeine masks drowsiness without lowering BAC; human liver ADH enzymes metabolize alcohol at a nearly fixed zero-order rate (~0.015%/hr), meaning only elapsed time restores sobriety.
