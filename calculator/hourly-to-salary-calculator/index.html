How It Works & Mathematical Principles

An hourly-to-salary calculator converts an hourly wage rate into equivalent annual, monthly, biweekly, and weekly gross pay based on actual weekly hours and paid weeks per year.

Comparing a $35/hr contract or hourly offer against a salaried position requires normalizing work schedules. While 40 hours × 52 weeks (2,080 hours) serves as the standard full-time benchmark, adjusting weeks worked for unpaid time off gives a realistic annual gross comparison.

Governing Equation
Annual Salary = Hourly Rate × Hours/Week × Weeks/Year

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Converting a $35/hour wage working 40 hours per week for 52 paid weeks.

  • Total annual paid hours: 40 × 52 = 2,080 hours.
  • Gross annual compensation: $35.00 × 2,080 = $72,800/yr.
  • Monthly equivalent ($72,800 ÷ 12) = $6,066.67/mo; weekly = $1,400.00/wk.

Result: $72,800 / year ($6,066.67 / month)

Quick mental rule: doubling the hourly rate in thousands ($35 -> $70k) approximates a full-time 2,000-hour annual wage; multiplying by 2,080 yields the exact 52-week figure.

Using 52 Weeks for Freelance or Unpaid-PTO Hourly Roles

If two weeks of holidays and vacation are unpaid, calculating at 52 weeks overstates annual take-home by roughly 4%.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is 2,080 hours used as the standard full-time year?

A standard full-time US work schedule consists of 40 hours per week multiplied by 52 weeks per year, which equals 2,080 paid hours annually.

How do I convert an hourly rate to a quick annual salary estimate?

As a quick mental rule of thumb for 40 hours/week across 52 weeks, double your hourly rate and add three zeros—for instance, $35/hour × 2 = $70,000 (~$72,800 exact).

Should I reduce paid weeks if I take unpaid time off?

Yes. Freelancers and contractors who take 2 to 4 unpaid weeks off for vacation or holidays should adjust Paid Weeks per Year to 48–50 for an accurate gross annual projection.
