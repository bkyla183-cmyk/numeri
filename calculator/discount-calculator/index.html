How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A discount and sale price calculator computes the exact markdown dollar savings and final checkout price from any original list price and percentage discount.

Instead of multiplying twice to subtract a discount, multiplying original price by the complement factor (1 − Discount%/100) produces the sale price in one step while clarifying exact dollars saved before sales tax.

Governing Equation
Savings = Original × (Discount% / 100) | Sale Price = Original - Savings

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Buying an $89.99 jacket during a 25% off store promotion.

  • Compute discount savings: $89.99 × 0.25 = $22.4975 -> $22.50 saved.
  • Subtract savings from sticker price: $89.99 - $22.50 = $67.49.

Result: $67.49 Final Sale Price ($22.50 saved)

You pay 75% of the sticker price ($67.49) prior to local sales tax.

Adding Multiple Promotional Percentages Together

Applying "25% off plus an extra 10% off" yields 32.5% total off rather than 35% because the second markdown applies to the already-reduced price.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you stack two sequential store discounts like 20% off plus an extra 15%?

Sequential discounts multiply rather than add: remaining price equals Original × (1 − 0.20) × (1 − 0.15) = 0.68 × Original, which is a 32% total discount rather than 35%.

Should discounts be applied before or after sales tax?

In standard US/UK retail pricing, percentage store discounts apply to the pre-tax price first, and sales tax is calculated afterward on the lower discounted price.

How do you calculate the amount saved on a sale item?

Multiply Original Price × (Discount % ÷ 100) to find dollars saved, then subtract that figure from the original price.
