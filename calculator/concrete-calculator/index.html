How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Concrete Slab Volume Calculator computes total cubic yards, cubic feet, and 60 lb or 80 lb premix concrete bags needed for slabs, patios, sidewalks, and footings from length, width, and thickness in inches.

Concrete ready-mix trucks sell by the cubic yard (27 cubic feet), while hardware stores stock 60 lb (0.45 cu ft) and 80 lb (0.60 cu ft) premix bags. Converting slab thickness from inches to feet accurately prevents costly mid-pour shortages.

Governing Equation
Cubic Yards = [Length (ft) × Width (ft) × (Depth (in) / 12)] / 27

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Pouring a 20 ft × 10 ft residential patio slab at 4 inches thick.

  • Compute cubic feet: 20 × 10 × (4 / 12) = 66.67 cu ft.
  • Divide by 27 cu ft/yd³: 66.67 / 27 = 2.47 Cubic Yards.
  • Premix bag equivalents: 112 bags (60 lb @ 0.60/0.45) or 84 bags (80 lb @ ~0.60 cu ft/bag, rounded up).

Result: 2.47 Cubic Yards (66.7 cu ft / 84 × 80 lb bags)

Ordering ~2.75 cubic yards (including a 10% overage allowance) protects against uneven sub-base grading and chute waste.

Forgetting to Divide Inches of Thickness by 12 Before Multiplying

Multiplying 20 × 10 × 4 directly produces 12× too much concrete!

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are concrete ready-mix trucks ordered in cubic yards instead of cubic feet?

A cubic yard measures 3 ft × 3 ft × 3 ft = 27 cubic feet. Dividing (Length × Width × Thickness in feet) by 27 converts slab volume directly into standard North American ready-mix truck units.

How many 80-lb premix concrete bags make 1 cubic yard?

One standard 80-lb concrete bag yields roughly 0.60 cubic feet, so 1 cubic yard (27 cu ft) requires 45 bags of 80-lb concrete (or about 60 bags of 60-lb mix).

Should I add a waste buffer before ordering concrete?

Yes. Contractors routinely add 5%–10% extra concrete volume to cover uneven subgrade excavation, form deflection, and mixer chute leftovers.
