How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Fuel Economy Converter translates vehicle efficiency smoothly among US Miles Per Gallon (US MPG), UK Imperial MPG, and metric Liters per 100 Kilometers (L/100km).

Metric fuel consumption (L/100km) is an inverse scale compared with MPG: lower L/100km is better, whereas higher MPG is better. Furthermore, because the UK Imperial gallon (4.546 L) is ~20% larger than the US liquid gallon (3.785 L), UK MPG specifications always look ~20% higher than identical US EPA window-sticker numbers.

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Converting an EPA-rated 30 US MPG sedan into metric L/100km and UK Imperial MPG.

  • Compute metric consumption: 235.214583 / 30 = 7.8405 -> 7.84 L/100km.
  • Scale to UK Imperial gallon: 30 × 1.20095 = 36.03 UK MPG.

Result: 7.84 L / 100km (36.03 UK MPG)

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the relationship between MPG and L/100km inverse rather than linear?

MPG measures distance per unit of fuel, whereas L/100km measures fuel per unit of distance. Converting between them uses L/100km = 235.215 ÷ US MPG.

Why does upgrading from 15 MPG to 20 MPG save more gas than upgrading from 40 MPG to 50 MPG?

Over 10,000 miles, moving from 15 to 20 MPG cuts fuel used from 667 gallons to 500 gallons (saving 167 gallons), while 40 to 50 MPG drops fuel used from 250 to 200 gallons (saving 50 gallons).

Why are UK MPG figures roughly 20% higher than US MPG figures?

The UK Imperial gallon contains 4.54609 liters compared with the smaller 3.78541-liter US gallon, so the exact same car travels about 20.1% farther per Imperial gallon.
