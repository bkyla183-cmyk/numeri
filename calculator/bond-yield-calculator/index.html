How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Bond Yield to Maturity (YTM) & Duration Calculator solves exact annualized Yield to Maturity, Current Yield, Effective Annual Yield (EAY), Macaulay Duration, Modified Duration interest-rate sensitivity, and Tax-Equivalent Municipal Yield across Semi-Annual, Annual, and Quarterly coupon bonds.

Buying a $1,000 par bond with a 5.0% coupon at a discount price of $940 earns MORE than its 5.319% Current Yield because you also collect a $60 capital gain upon maturity. Discounting all 20 semi-annual coupons plus the $1,000 redemption value back to $940 solves exact YTM (5.799%) and quantifies Modified Duration (7.69% price drop per +1% yield jump).

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A 10-year $1,000 par Treasury/corporate bond paying a 5.0% semi-annual coupon ($25 every 6 mos) currently trading at $940.00 (Discount Bond; 24% marginal tax rate).

  • Semi-annual coupon = ($1,000 × 5%) ÷ 2 = $25.00 across 20 periods. Current Yield = $50 ÷ $940 = 5.319%.
  • Solving exact price equation at $940 yields r_per = 2.8997% per 6 months → Bond-Equivalent YTM = 5.799% (5.883% Effective Annual Yield).
  • PV-weighted cash-flow timing yields Macaulay Duration = 7.91 years → Modified Duration = 7.91 ÷ 1.029 = 7.69% (+1% yield jump ≈ -7.69% bond price drop).

Result: 5.799% Annualized YTM | 5.319% Current Yield | 7.69% Modified Duration

Because the bond trades below par ($940 < $1,000), YTM (5.799%) exceeds both the coupon rate (5.00%) and Current Yield (5.319%).

Confusing Current Yield With Yield to Maturity on Premium Bonds

A high-coupon bond trading at $1,120 can advertise a tempting Current Yield while delivering a much lower YTM once the $120 capital loss at maturity is factored in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a bond’s Coupon Rate, Current Yield, and Yield to Maturity (YTM)?

Coupon Rate is fixed interest relative to par ($1,000); Current Yield divides annual coupon dollars by current market price; and Yield to Maturity (YTM) solves the full annualized IRR capturing both coupons and any capital gain or loss from holding to maturity.

How does Modified Duration estimate bond price sensitivity to interest-rate shifts?

Modified Duration approximates the percentage price move for a 1.00% (100 bps) shift in market yields: ΔPrice% ≈ −ModDur × ΔYield. A bond with 7.69% Modified Duration drops roughly 7.69% if yields jump +1.00%.

When is Municipal Tax-Equivalent Yield useful?

Tax-Equivalent Yield [YTM ÷ (1 − Marginal Tax Rate)] converts tax-exempt municipal bond returns into the pre-tax yield a taxable corporate or Treasury bond must offer to leave an investor with the same after-tax income.
