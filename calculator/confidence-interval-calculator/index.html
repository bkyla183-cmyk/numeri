How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A confidence interval calculator computes exact lower and upper bounds at 90%, 95%, 99%, or 99.9% confidence for population means using Student’s t* (when sample SD s is observed) or Normal Z* (when σ is known), as well as sample proportions (Wald & Wilson Score).

Because sample averages fluctuate from draw to draw, reporting a single point estimate hides sampling uncertainty. Dividing standard deviation by √n produces the Standard Error, and scaling by Student’s t*(df = n − 1) properly accounts for extra variance estimation uncertainty before multiplying.

Governing Equation
Mean CI = x̄ ± t*(df) · (s / √n) | Known σ: x̄ ± Z* · (σ / √n) | Proportion: p̂ ± Z* · √[p̂(1-p̂)/n]

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Constructing a 95% confidence interval for sample mean 50, sample SD s = 8, and sample size n = 64 (df = 63).

  • Compute Standard Error: SE = 8 / √64 = 8 / 8 = 1.000.
  • Look up Student’s t* for df = 63 at 95% confidence: t* = 1.9983 (vs. Z* = 1.960 if σ were known).
  • Compute Margin of Error & Bounds: ME = 1.9983 × 1.000 = ±1.998 ⇒ [48.002, 51.998].

Result: [48.002, 51.998] (Student t* = 1.9983, Margin of Error = ±1.998)

Using Student’s t* captures added uncertainty from estimating σ via sample s; switching Basis to Population σ (Z* = 1.960) narrows the interval slightly to [48.040, 51.960].

Forgetting to Divide Standard Deviation by √n

Multiplying Z* directly by raw s instead of the standard error s/√n computes a sample tolerance range rather than a confidence interval for the mean.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 95% confidence interval mean there is a 95% chance the true mean is inside this specific interval?

Strictly in frequentist statistics, the true population mean is fixed: 95% confidence means that if you repeated the same random sampling procedure many times, 95% of the resulting intervals would capture the true population mean.

How do you cut a poll’s margin of error in half?

Because margin of error scales with 1/√n, cutting the margin of error in half requires multiplying your sample size n by four (4×), assuming the same confidence level and variance.

Why does a 99% confidence interval look wider than a 90% interval—and when does Student t* apply?

Higher confidence requires a larger critical value (e.g., Z* = 2.576 vs. 1.645, or 3.291 at 99.9%), widening the interval. When only the sample standard deviation s is known, the calculator uses Student’s t*(df = n − 1), which is slightly wider than Z* for small or moderate samples.
