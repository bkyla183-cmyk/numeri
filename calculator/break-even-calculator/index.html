How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A break-even calculator determines the exact unit sales volume and revenue required for a business or product line to cover all fixed overhead and per-unit variable costs, reaching zero net loss.

Every unit sold above variable cost contributes a fixed dollar slice—called the Contribution Margin—toward paying down rent, salaries, software, and equipment leases. Once total contribution matches fixed costs, every subsequent unit generates direct operating profit.

Governing Equation
Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price - Variable Cost per Unit)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A small manufacturer carries $24,000 in monthly fixed costs, sells each unit for $80, and pays $35 per unit in direct variable costs.

  • Unit Contribution Margin = $80 - $35 = $45 per unit.
  • Divide fixed overhead by contribution margin: $24,000 / $45 = 533.33 units.
  • Round up to whole units: 534 units ($42,720 break-even revenue).

Result: 534 Units ($42,720 Break-Even Revenue)

Selling the 534th unit eliminates fixed overhead; each unit sold from #535 onward adds $45 directly to operating profit.

Omitting Payment Processing & Shipping From Variable Costs

Leaving out 3% card fees or outbound freight overstates unit contribution margin and understates required break-even sales.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is unit contribution margin in break-even analysis?

Contribution margin per unit equals Selling Price minus Variable Cost per unit. Every unit sold contributes that amount toward paying down fixed overhead costs until total fixed costs are covered.

Why does the break-even calculator round units up to the next whole number?

You cannot typically sell a fraction of a finished physical product. If fixed costs require 533.33 units to break even, selling 533 still leaves a small net loss, so 534 units is the true break-even threshold.

What happens if variable cost exceeds the selling price?

Unit contribution margin turns negative, meaning each unit sold loses money and the business can never reach break-even by increasing sales volume alone without raising price or cutting unit costs.
