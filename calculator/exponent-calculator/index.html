How It Works & Mathematical Principles

An exponent calculator computes powers x^y for positive, negative, zero, and fractional exponents—making exponential growth, scientific scaling, and fractional root evaluations immediate.

Exponentiation extends repeated multiplication across all real numbers: negative exponents invert bases (x^(-n) = 1 / x^n), zero exponents equal 1 (for x ≠ 0), and fractional exponents express roots such as x^(1/2) = √x.

Confusing -2^2 With (-2)^2

Standard operator precedence evaluates -2^2 as -(2^2) = -4, whereas (-2)^2 = +4.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a negative exponent mean?

A negative exponent denotes the reciprocal of the positive power: x^(−n) = 1 / x^n for non-zero x. For example, 2^(−3) = 1 / 2³ = 1/8 = 0.125.

Why does any non-zero number raised to the 0 power equal 1?

By the quotient rule x^a ÷ x^a = x^(a−a) = x⁰, and any non-zero quantity divided by itself equals 1.

Can fractional exponents compute roots?

Yes. Raising a positive base to 1/n computes its n-th root—for instance, 16^0.5 equals √16 = 4, and 27^(1/3) equals ∛27 = 3.
