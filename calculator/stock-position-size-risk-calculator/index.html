How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Stock Position Size & Risk Calculator applies fixed-fractional account risk sizing to compute the exact maximum whole shares to trade—explicitly deducting round-trip Entry + Exit commissions from your dollar risk budget and evaluating reward-to-risk R-Multiples for both Long and Short setups.

Undisciplined traders size positions by dollar hunch ("let me buy $10,000 of Stock X") rather than stop-loss distance. Under Van Tharp / institutional fixed-fractional risk sizing, your dollar risk per share |Entry − Stop| determines position size so that whether a stock has a $1.00 or $10.00 stop distance, a stop-out loses the exact same controlled fraction (typically 0.5%–2.0%) of trading equity—after round-trip commissions.

Governing Equation
Max Shares = floor[(Balance × Risk% − Comm_in − Comm_out) ÷ |Entry − Stop|] | R-Multiple = |Target − Entry| ÷ |Entry − Stop|

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Sizing a Long trade in a $50,000 account risking 1.5% ($750 max loss), entering at $48.00 with a stop-loss at $44.50, target at $58.50, and $5 entry + $5 exit commissions.

  • Maximum Risk Budget = $50,000 × 1.5% = $750.00; Subtracting $5 entry + $5 exit commissions leaves $740.00 net price risk.
  • 1R Stop Distance per share = |$48.00 − $44.50| = $3.50/share.
  • Maximum Whole Shares = floor($740.00 ÷ $3.50) = floor(211.428) = 211 shares (Total Position Value = 211 × $48.00 = $10,128.00 = 20.26% of account equity).
  • Planned Total Risk Including Commissions = (211 × $3.50) + $10.00 = $748.50. Target $58.50 = +$10.50/sh ÷ $3.50 = 3.00R ($2,205.50 net profit after $10 fees).

Result: 211 Shares Max Size ($10,128 Position · 3.00R Reward-to-Risk)

If stopped out cleanly at $44.50, total loss including commissions is capped at $748.50 (1.497% of capital), whereas hitting target adds +$2,205.50 (+4.41% account return).

Sizing formula applies uniformly across global equities with local margin & settlement norms:

Ignoring Concentration Allocation When Using Very Tight Stop-Losses

A $0.20 stop on a $50 stock recommends 3,700 shares ($185,000 on a $50k account = 370% equity)—meaning an ordinary 5% overnight gap wipes out $9,250 instead of $750.

Divides risk budget net of explicit Entry and Exit commissions by absolute stop distance |Entry − Stop|, rounding down strictly to integer shares.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do separate Entry and Exit broker commissions affect position sizing?

Both Entry and Exit fees are subtracted directly from your maximum trade risk budget BEFORE dividing by |Entry − StopLoss| and rounding down via floor()—preventing fees from accidentally pushing trade risk over your % rule.

What is the R-Multiple convention shown in the results?

1R equals your initial per-share risk distance |Entry − StopLoss|. Reaching a target price 3× further from entry than your stop equals a 3.00R winner ($2,205.50 net profit after $10 round-trip commissions in the worked example).

Can a stock gap past my stop-loss and cause larger losses than modeled?

Yes. Stop-market orders do not guarantee fill price during earnings surprises, market halts, or overnight gaps—always monitor Account Allocation % alongside stop risk.
