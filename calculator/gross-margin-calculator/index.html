How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A gross margin calculator evaluates business profitability by measuring the percentage of revenue retained after subtracting direct Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), alongside dollar gross profit and markup percentage.

Gross margin and markup are frequently confused in pricing decisions despite measuring different denominators. Gross margin expresses profit as a share of selling price, whereas markup expresses profit as a share of product cost—so a 100% markup yields a 50% gross margin.

Governing Equation
Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS | Gross Margin % = (Gross Profit / Revenue) × 100

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A company generates $100,000 in product revenue with $60,000 in direct Cost of Goods Sold.

  • Subtract COGS from revenue: $100,000 - $60,000 = $40,000 gross profit.
  • Divide gross profit by revenue: $40,000 / $100,000 = 0.40 -> 40.0% Gross Margin.
  • Check markup on cost for comparison: $40,000 / $60,000 = 66.67% Markup.

Result: 40% Gross Margin ($40,000 gross profit; 66.7% markup)

Forty cents of every revenue dollar stays available to cover payroll, rent, marketing, taxes, and net profit.

Pricing Products Using Markup When Targeting a Gross Margin

Adding a 40% markup to a $60 unit yields an $84 price—resulting in only a 28.6% gross margin ($24/$84), not 40%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between gross margin and markup?

Gross margin divides gross profit by selling revenue ((Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue), so margin can never reach 100% if COGS is positive. Markup divides gross profit by cost ((Revenue − COGS) ÷ COGS) and can easily exceed 100%.

What counts toward Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)?

COGS includes direct raw materials, manufacturing labor, packaging, and inbound freight directly tied to producing goods sold—not overhead like office rent, marketing, or software subscriptions.

What is considered a healthy gross margin?

Target margins vary widely by industry: grocery and retail often run 20%–35%, manufacturing 30%–45%, and software/SaaS products commonly exceed 70%–85% due to low per-unit delivery costs.
