How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Corrected Calcium Calculator (Payne Equation) adjusts measured total serum calcium for hypoalbuminemia across both US Conventional (mg/dL, g/dL) and International SI (mmol/L, g/L) laboratory standards, estimating the physiologically active calcium level and approximate free ionized fraction.

Roughly 40%–45% of circulating total blood calcium is bound to plasma proteins—predominantly serum albumin—while ~50% circulates as biologically active free ionized calcium (Ca²⁺). When a patient has low serum albumin due to liver disease, malnutrition, nephrotic syndrome, or acute inflammation, total measured calcium looks artificially low even when free ionized calcium is normal. Payne’s 1973 linear adjustment adds 0.8 mg/dL per 1.0 g/dL drop below 4.0 g/dL albumin (or 0.02 mmol/L per 1 g/L below 40 g/L).

Governing Equation
US: Ca_corr = Ca_meas + 0.8 × (4.0 − Albumin g/dL) | SI: Ca_corr = Ca_meas + 0.02 × (40 − Albumin g/L)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A hospitalized patient has a measured serum calcium of 7.8 mg/dL (1.95 mmol/L) alongside hypoalbuminemia at 2.5 g/dL (25 g/L).

  • Calculate albumin deficit below reference: 4.0 − 2.5 = 1.5 g/dL.
  • Apply Payne factor: 0.8 × 1.5 = +1.20 mg/dL calcium adjustment.
  • Add adjustment to measured calcium: 7.8 + 1.20 = 9.00 mg/dL (2.25 mmol/L SI; ~1.12 mmol/L free ionized approximation).

Result: 9.00 mg/dL Corrected Calcium (2.25 mmol/L · Normocalcemia 8.5–10.5 mg/dL)

What initially appeared to be hypocalcemia (7.8 mg/dL) corrects cleanly into the normal physiological range (9.00 mg/dL), helping prevent unnecessary calcium supplementation.

Regional Standards & Units

Supports instant switching between US Conventional lab reporting and International SI chemistry units:

  • United States: Total calcium in mg/dL (normal ~8.5–10.5 mg/dL); serum albumin in g/dL (normal ~3.5–5.0 g/dL). (mg/dL & g/dL) [2026 Clinical Lab]
  • UK / Canada / Australia / NZ: SI reporting in mmol/L calcium (normal ~2.12–2.62 mmol/L) and g/L albumin (normal ~35–50 g/L). (mmol/L & g/L) [2026 NHS / RCPA]

Correcting Upward When Albumin Is Elevated Above 4.0 g/dL or Ignoring pH

Applying Payne formulas blindly during dehydration (high albumin) or acute respiratory alkalosis can mask true ionized hypocalcemia. Confirm clinically with direct ionized Ca²⁺.

Authoritative Sources & References

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do clinicians correct total serum calcium for low albumin?

Approximately 40%–45% of blood calcium is bound to serum albumin, while only free ionized calcium (~50%) is physiologically active. When albumin drops below 4.0 g/dL (40 g/L), measured total calcium falls artificially even though ionized calcium may still be completely normal—Payne’s equation adjusts +0.8 mg/dL per 1.0 g/dL albumin deficit.

How do US Conventional units (mg/dL, g/dL) convert to UK/CA/AU SI units (mmol/L, g/L)?

Switch the Laboratory Unit Standard dropdown between US and SI: 1 mmol/L calcium equals 4.008 mg/dL, and 1 g/dL albumin equals 10 g/L. The SI Payne formula adds 0.02 mmol/L for every 1 g/L drop below 40 g/L albumin.

When is the Payne calcium correction unreliable?

In critically ill ICU patients, severe chronic kidney disease, acute acid-base shifts (alkalosis increases protein binding), or multiple myeloma, albumin-corrected formulas diverge from true free calcium. Order a direct blood-gas ionized calcium (Ca²⁺) test in those settings.
