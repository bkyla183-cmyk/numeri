How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The CAGR Calculator determines the true Compound Annual Growth Rate connecting an initial investment value and ending value across whole or fractional years, reporting Gross CAGR alongside multiplicative annual fee drag V_f(1 − f)^n and Net-of-Fee-and-Tax CAGR.

Simple average return (Total Return ÷ Years) ignores compounding and consistently overstates multi-year annualized growth. Additionally, real fund expense ratios or AUM management fees compound multiplicatively against portfolio value each year rather than via naive subtraction. Using V_after_fee = V_f × (1 − f)^n reconciles ending dollar portfolio values identically with Net CAGR = (1 + CAGR_gross)(1 − f) − 1.

Governing Equation
Gross CAGR = (V_f ÷ V₀)^(1/n) − 1 | V_after_fee = V_f(1 − f)^n | Net CAGR = (1 + CAGR_g)(1 − f) − 1

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Evaluating an investment that grew from $10,000 to $24,500 gross over 8 years with a 0.75% annual expense ratio and 15% exit capital gains tax.

  • Gross CAGR = ($24,500 ÷ $10,000)^(1 ÷ 8) − 1 = (2.45)^(0.125) − 1 = 11.8525%/yr (Total gross return = 145.00%).
  • Multiplicative Annual Fee (0.75%/yr): Ending Value After Fee = $24,500.00 × (1 − 0.0075)^8 = $24,500 × 0.9415515 = $23,068.01.
  • Net-of-Fee CAGR = (1.118525 × 0.9925) − 1 = 11.0136%/yr [verifies: ($23,068.01 ÷ $10,000)^(1/8) − 1 = 11.0136%].
  • Exit Capital Gains Tax (15% on positive gain $13,068.01) = $1,960.20 → Net Ending Value = $21,107.81 (Net-of-All CAGR = 9.7881%/yr).

Result: 11.8525% Gross CAGR (11.0136% Net of Fees · $23,068.01 After Fee · $21,107.81 After Tax)

Even an ordinary-sounding 0.75% annual AUM fee consumes $1,431.99 of final wealth across 8 years, and combined fee + exit tax drag totals $3,392.19.

Regional Standards & Units

Mathematical CAGR is universal globally while capital gains taxation on exit differs by jurisdiction:

  • United States: Long-term >1-year capital gains taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% federally (+3.8% NIIT above thresholds). (USD ($)) [2026]
  • United Kingdom: CGT applies outside ISA/SIPP wrappers above annual exempt allowance (£3,000). (GBP (£)) [2026–27]
  • Canada: Non-registered capital gains use statutory inclusion rate outside TFSA/RRSP. (CAD (C$)) [2026]
  • Australia: Assets held >12 months typically receive 50% CGT discount for individuals. (AUD (A$)) [FY 2026–27]

Treating CAGR as Stable Year-by-Year Return—or Adding Arithmetic Percentages

Two assets with identical 11.85% CAGR can experience wildly different drawdowns along the path; additionally, multiplying fee linearly instead of (1 − f)^n misstates cumulative expense drag.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this CAGR calculator deduct an annual AUM or fund expense ratio?

Using standard multiplicative compounding: Ending Value After Fee = V_f × (1 − f)^n, which matches Net-of-Fee CAGR = (1 + Gross CAGR)(1 − f) − 1 identically to the cent.

Why is CAGR always equal to or lower than simple arithmetic average return?

Compounded wealth suffers asymmetric volatility drag (e.g., +50% followed by −50% leaves you at 75%, giving 0% arithmetic mean but −13.4% CAGR). CAGR measures exact geometric compounded performance.

Is CAGR suitable if I added or withdrew cash mid-horizon?

Basic CAGR assumes no mid-period cash flows between Day 0 and Year n. If making recurring monthly contributions, use our Dollar-Cost Averaging or DRIP calculators instead.
