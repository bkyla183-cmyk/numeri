How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Value Added Tax (VAT) calculator both adds VAT onto net B2B/service prices and reverse-extracts embedded VAT from gross tax-inclusive retail receipts across UK, European, and international VAT rates.

Unlike US sales tax added at checkout, consumer prices across the UK, EU, Australia (GST), and Gulf states are typically displayed VAT-inclusive. Calculating net revenue on a €240 gross invoice at 20% VAT requires dividing by 1.20 rather than subtracting 20%.

Governing Equation
Gross = Net × (1 + VAT%) | Net (Extracted) = Gross / (1 + VAT%)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Adding a 20% standard UK VAT rate onto a £200 (or $200) net consulting invoice.

  • Compute VAT payable: $200 × 0.20 = $40.00.
  • Compute VAT-inclusive gross invoice: $200 × 1.20 = $240.00.
  • Reverse extraction check: $240.00 / 1.20 = $200.00 net ($40.00 VAT).

Result: $240.00 Gross Invoice ($40.00 VAT)

Notice that VAT makes up 16.67% of the gross $240 total even though the statutory VAT rate is 20% of the net $200 base.

Multiplying a VAT-Inclusive Receipt by 20% to Find Included Tax

Taking 20% of £240 yields £48 instead of the true £40 VAT; always divide by (1 + rate) first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I add VAT to a net price vs. extract VAT from a gross invoice?

To add VAT, multiply Net × (1 + VAT%). To reverse-extract VAT from a gross total, divide Gross ÷ (1 + VAT%) to find the net subtotal—do not multiply Gross directly by the VAT rate.

What are standard VAT rates in the UK and EU?

The UK standard VAT rate is 20% (with reduced 5% and 0% tiers for essentials), while European Union standard VAT rates generally range from 17% to 27% depending on the member state.

What is the difference between US sales tax and Value-Added Tax (VAT)?

US sales tax is typically added at the final retail point of sale on top of sticker prices, whereas VAT is collected at each production stage and generally included directly in displayed European retail prices.
