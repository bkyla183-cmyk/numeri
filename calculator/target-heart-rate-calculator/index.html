How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Target Heart Rate Calculator computes five aerobic and anaerobic cardiovascular training zones using the Tanaka maximum heart rate formula paired with the Karvonen Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) method.

Unlike basic age-only percentages, the Karvonen method accounts for your Resting Heart Rate—tailoring Zone 2 fat-burning and Zone 4 threshold intervals to your actual cardiovascular conditioning.

Governing Equation
MHR (Tanaka) = 208 - 0.7 × Age | THR = RHR + (MHR - RHR) × Intensity%

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Calculating Zone 2–3 aerobic training pulse for a 30-year-old athlete with a resting heart rate of 65 bpm.

  • Tanaka Max Heart Rate = 208 - (0.7 × 30) = 187 bpm.
  • Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) = 187 - 65 = 122 bpm.
  • Moderate Zone 2–3 (60%–70% HRR): Lower = 65 + (122 × 0.60) = 138 bpm; Upper = 65 + (122 × 0.70) = 150 bpm.

Result: 138 – 150 bpm Aerobic Target Zone (Max HR ≈ 187 bpm)

Training consistently around 138–150 bpm builds mitochondrial endurance efficiently without excessive recovery fatigue.

Measuring Resting Heart Rate Immediately After Coffee or Commuting

Take your RHR right after waking up before standing or consuming caffeine for an accurate baseline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the Karvonen method differ from simple % of Max Heart Rate?

Karvonen uses Heart Rate Reserve (HRR = Max HR − Resting HR), scaling workout intensity between your actual resting baseline and maximum ceiling rather than assuming everyone shares the same resting heart rate.

Why does the Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × Age) replace "220 − Age"?

Meta-analyses by Hirofumi Tanaka showed the classic 220 − Age rule tends to overestimate maximum heart rate in younger adults and underestimate it in older adults.

Which heart rate zone targets aerobic endurance vs. peak anaerobic intervals?

Zone 2 (60%–70% HRR) develops foundational aerobic capacity and mitochondrial endurance, while Zone 4–5 (80%–100% HRR) trains lactate threshold and maximal VO₂ capacity.
