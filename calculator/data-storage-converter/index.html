How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Data Storage Converter converts digital capacity across Bits, Bytes, Kilobytes, Megabytes, Gigabytes, Terabytes, and Petabytes under binary IEC (1,024-based) memory sizing conventions.

Operating systems (Windows, macOS memory utilities) generally calculate file and RAM sizes in powers of 2 (1,024 = 2¹⁰ per step, formally IEC KiB/MiB/GiB/TiB), whereas hard drive manufacturers label drives using powers of 10 (1,000 per SI step)—which is why a "1 TB" drive displays around 931 GiB when formatted.

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Converting 100 GB (binary 1,024 GiB basis) into MB, TB, and Bytes.

  • Multiply by 1,024 to obtain MB: 100 × 1,024 = 102,400 MB.
  • Divide by 1,024 to obtain TB: 100 / 1,024 = 0.0977 TB.

Result: 102,400 MB (0.0977 TB)

Keep the ~7.3% gap between decimal SI (10⁹) drive stickers and binary OS GiB (2³⁰) in mind when sizing RAID arrays or cloud buckets.

Dividing File Download Time by Megabits Without Converting to Bytes

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my 1 TB hard drive show only ~931 GB in Windows?

Storage manufacturers advertise capacities in decimal SI units (1 TB = 10¹² bytes), while Windows reports GiB/TiB-sized binary powers of 1024 (2⁴⁰ bytes), making 1,000⁴ ÷ 1,024³ ≈ 931.32 GiB.

What is the difference between a bit and a byte?

One byte equals 8 binary bits. Internet speeds are typically quoted in megabits per second (Mbps), while file sizes are measured in megabytes (MB)—so a 100 Mbps connection downloads at up to 12.5 MB/s.

What are IEC binary prefixes (KiB, MiB, GiB, TiB)?

Defined by the IEC, kibibyte (KiB = 1024 B), mebibyte (MiB = 1024² B), and gibibyte (GiB = 1024³ B) eliminate ambiguity against decimal SI prefixes (1000, 1000², 1000³).
