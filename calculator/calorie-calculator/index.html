How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Calorie & TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) calculator estimates your daily caloric burn at rest (BMR) using the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and scales it by lifestyle activity level to guide maintenance, fat loss, or lean muscle gain targets.

TDEE combines Basal Metabolic Rate (the energy needed for breathing, heartbeat, and cellular maintenance) with daily non-exercise movement (NEAT), exercise, and food thermogenesis. Establishing your maintenance baseline prevents counterproductive crash dieting by anchoring deficits above clinical minimums.

Governing Equation
BMR = 10·W(kg) + 6.25·H(cm) - 5·A(yrs) + (+5 men / -161 women); TDEE = BMR × Activity

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Estimating daily maintenance calories for a 30-year-old male weighing 75 kg at 178 cm with light activity (1.375).

  • Compute BMR: (10 × 75) + (6.25 × 178) - (5 × 30) + 5 = 750 + 1,112.5 - 150 + 5 = 1,717.5 kcal.
  • Multiply by activity factor: 1,717.5 × 1.375 = 2,361.56 -> 2,362 kcal/day.

Result: 2,362 kcal / day Maintenance TDEE

Consuming ~1,862 kcal/day yields a sustainable ~500 kcal daily deficit (~1 lb/week fat loss) safely above clinical floors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) measures calories burned solely to keep vital organs functioning at complete rest, whereas TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) multiplies BMR by an activity factor covering daily movement and exercise.

Why does Numeriq use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation?

Clinical validation studies (including American Dietetic Association reviews) rank Mifflin-St Jeor as the most consistently accurate predictive equation for modern non-athlete populations when lean mass is unknown.

How large should a sustainable daily calorie deficit be?

A modest deficit of 300–500 kcal/day below TDEE typically targets gradual fat loss of roughly 0.5 to 1 lb (0.25–0.45 kg) per week while preserving muscle mass and energy.
