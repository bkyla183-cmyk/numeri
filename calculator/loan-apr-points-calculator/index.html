How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Mortgage Points & Effective APR Calculator quantifies upfront discount points (1 point = 1% of loan amount), monthly P&I rate-buydown savings, exact break-even month to recover points, and True Truth-in-Lending (TILA) Effective APR.

Paying $8,000 (2 discount points) at closing to buy a 6.75% mortgage rate down to 6.125% saves $163.95 every month—but takes 49 months (4.1 years) simply to break even. If you sell or refinance in Year 3, buying points destroys wealth. This tool solves both your exact break-even month and Regulation Z Effective APR.

Governing Equation
PointsCost = Loan × (Pts ÷ 100) | BreakEven = PointsCost ÷ ΔPMT | TILA_APR = IRR(Loan − Points − Fees)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Evaluating 2.0 discount points ($8,000) + $1,800 lender fees on a $400,000 30-year mortgage to buy the par rate down from 6.75% to 6.125%.

  • Points Cost = $400,000 × 2% = $8,000.00; Total Prepaid Finance Charge = $9,800.00 (TILA Amount Financed = $390,200.00).
  • Monthly P&I drops from $2,594.39 (6.75%) to $2,430.44 (6.125%) → Monthly Savings = $163.95/mo.
  • Simple Break-Even = $8,000 ÷ $163.95 = 48.8 months (Month 49 / 4.1 yrs).
  • True TILA Effective APR with points drops to 6.360% vs. Par Effective APR of 6.794% (+$51,022.08 net savings if kept all 30 years).

Result: 6.360% True TILA Effective APR | 49-Month Break-Even ($163.95/mo Saved)

Buying 2 points makes strong financial sense ONLY if you keep this mortgage past Month 49 (4.1 years) without selling or refinancing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a mortgage discount point and how much does one point cost?

One discount point equals 1% of the mortgage loan amount paid upfront at closing (e.g., $4,000 on a $400,000 loan) in exchange for a permanently reduced interest rate—typically 0.25% to 0.375% lower depending on lender pricing.

When does paying upfront mortgage points actually backfire?

If you sell the home or refinance into lower rates before reaching your break-even month (Points Cost ÷ Monthly P&I Savings), your upfront cash outlay exceeds cumulative monthly savings, making the Effective APR higher than taking the zero-point par rate.

How does Regulation Z calculate Effective APR with discount points?

Under federal Truth-in-Lending rules, discount points and prepaid lender origination charges are subtracted from the gross loan principal to determine the Net Amount Financed, and the IRR equating future monthly P&I payments to that net sum yields the Effective APR.
