How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A College GPA Calculator computes both semester GPA and cumulative Grade Point Average on the standard US 4.0 credit-weighted scale, factoring prior accumulated credits and cumulative GPA.

Not all courses weigh equally toward GPA: a 4-credit science lab carries double the impact of a 2-credit seminar. Multiplying each letter-grade point value by course credits yields quality points, which combine cleanly with prior semester totals.

Governing Equation
GPA = [Σ(Grade Points_i × Credits_i) + (Prior GPA × Prior Credits)] / (ΣCredits_i + Prior Credits)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Evaluating a 10-credit semester (3cr A [4.0], 4cr B+ [3.3], 3cr A- [3.7]) on top of 30 prior credits at a 3.40 cumulative GPA.

  • Semester quality points: (3 × 4.0) + (4 × 3.3) + (3 × 3.7) = 12.0 + 13.2 + 11.1 = 36.3 across 10 credits (Semester GPA = 3.63).
  • Prior quality points: 3.40 × 30 = 102.0 points.
  • Cumulative GPA: (36.3 + 102.0) / (10 + 30) = 138.3 / 40 = 3.4575 -> 3.46.

Result: 3.46 Cumulative GPA (3.63 Semester GPA across 40 Total Credits)

Even a strong 3.63 semester raises cumulative GPA modestly (+0.06) once 30 prior credits anchor the average.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a weighted credit-hour GPA calculated?

Multiply each course’s grade point value (on the 4.0 scale) by its credit hours to get quality points, sum all quality points, and divide by total attempted credit hours.

How do prior cumulative credits combine with this semester’s courses?

Multiply your prior GPA by prior credits to find existing quality points, add this term’s new quality points, and divide by (Prior Credits + New Credits).

Why do high-credit lab or studio courses affect GPA more than 1-credit seminars?

Because credit hours act as weights: a 4-credit science course carries four times the quality-point impact of a 1-credit elective.
