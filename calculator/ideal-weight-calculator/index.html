How It Works & Mathematical Principles

An Ideal Body Weight (IBW) Calculator evaluates Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi clinical formulas based on height and biological sex alongside the healthy 18.5–24.9 BMI weight span.

Originally created for pharmacological dosing in hospitals, classic IBW equations benchmark height above 5 feet (60 inches). Presenting the healthy BMI range alongside Devine’s equation highlights why healthy body mass spans a natural zone rather than a single rigid pound target.

Governing Equation
Devine (Men): 50.0 + 2.3 × (Height_in - 60) kg | Devine (Women): 45.5 + 2.3 × (Height_in - 60) kg

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Computing Devine Ideal Body Weight for a 175 cm (68.90 in) adult male.

  • Inches over 5 feet (60 in): 68.898 - 60 = 8.898 inches.
  • Add 2.3 kg per inch above 60 in: 50 + (2.3 × 8.898) = 50 + 20.46 = 70.5 kg (~155 lbs).

Result: 70.5 kg (~155 lbs) Devine IBW

Notice that at 175 cm, a healthy WHO BMI (18.5–24.9) spans ~56.7 kg to 76.3 kg, giving wide natural accommodation for frame size and muscle mass.

Treating a Single IBW Number as the Only Healthy Target

Treat IBW as the midpoint of a broader healthy range shaped by bone structure, genetics, and lean body mass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Devine, Robinson, Miller, and Hamwi formulas give slightly different weights?

Each clinical equation was fitted to different historical actuarial and clinical cohorts between the 1960s and 1980s, originally developed by physicians (such as B.J. Devine) for pharmacokinetic drug dosing rather than aesthetics.

Do ideal weight formulas apply below 5 feet (60 inches) in height?

Traditional equations benchmark inches above 5 feet (60 inches); for statures under 5 feet, predictive accuracy drops and clinical assessment with BMI or body composition is preferable.

What is a healthy way to interpret Ideal Body Weight outputs?

Treat the range across all four formulas alongside a healthy BMI interval as a general reference zone rather than a rigid target number, accounting for muscle mass and bone frame.
