How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A Cooking Volume Converter translates kitchen measurements cleanly across US cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, fluid ounces, and metric milliliters (mL) for scaling recipes accurately.

Baking and sauce scaling frequently require jumping between fractional US cup spoons and metric grams/milliliters printed on European recipes. Using exact culinary volume equivalents (1 US cup = 16 tablespoons = 48 teaspoons = 236.588 mL) prevents ruined doughs or overly salty reductions.

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Scaling 1.5 US cups into tablespoons, teaspoons, fluid ounces, and milliliters.

  • Tablespoons: 1.5 × 16 = 24 tablespoons.
  • Teaspoons: 1.5 × 48 = 72 teaspoons.
  • Fluid ounces & milliliters: 1.5 × 8 = 12 fl oz; 1.5 × 236.588 = 354.9 mL.

Result: 24 Tablespoons (72 tsp / 12 fl oz / 354.9 mL)

Notice how metric legal nutrition cups (240 mL) run slightly above exact US customary cups (236.59 mL), negligible in soups yet noticeable in large bakery batches.

Confusing Fluid Ounces (Volume) With Dry Ounces (Weight)

8 fluid ounces of water weighs 8 ounces, whereas 8 fl oz (1 cup) of sifted flour weighs only ~4.25 dry ounces.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many teaspoons and tablespoons are in 1 US cup?

Under standard US culinary conversions, 1 cup = 16 tablespoons = 48 teaspoons = 8 US fluid ounces (approximately 236.59 mL).

Why is US metric tablespoon 15 mL while US customary is ~14.79 mL?

Food nutrition labeling (FDA/USDA) defines a convenient 15 mL metric tablespoon and 240 mL cup, whereas US customary liquid volume derives 14.7868 mL and 236.588 mL from the 231-cubic-inch gallon.

Does weight in grams always convert identically for flour, sugar, and water?

No. Milliliters measure volume while grams measure mass. Because 1 mL of water weighs 1 g while 1 cup of sifted flour weighs roughly 120–125 g vs. ~200 g for sugar, only convert density-specifically to weight.
