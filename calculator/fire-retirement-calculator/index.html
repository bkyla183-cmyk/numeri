How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The FIRE Retirement Calculator computes your exact Financial Independence number using the Trinity / Bengen Safe Withdrawal Rate framework, Fisher inflation-adjusted net real return, Years to FIRE, Lean/Fat FIRE tiers, and your Coast FIRE milestone.

Planning early retirement on inflated future nominal dollars creates money illusion because a $1.375 million portfolio 20 years from now buys much less than it does today. By converting expected nominal return net of fund/tax drag into a real return using Fisher’s exact ratio [(1 + r_net)/(1 + i)] − 1, every figure—from Target FIRE Number to Lean/Fat FIRE and Coast FIRE—stays cleanly expressed in today’s constant purchasing power.

Governing Equation
FIRE Target = Annual Expenses ÷ (SWR ÷ 100) | r_real = (1 + r_nom − drag) ÷ (1 + i) − 1 | t = ln[(Target·r_real + S)/(P₀·r_real + S)] ÷ ln(1 + r_real)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A 32-year-old with $120,000 invested, $55,000 annual retirement spending, and $2,500 monthly savings modeling 8.0% nominal return, 0.5% fee/tax drag, 2.5% inflation, and a 4.0% Safe Withdrawal Rate.

  • FIRE Target = $55,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,375,000.00 (Lean FIRE 75%: $1,031,250.00; Fat FIRE 150%: $2,062,500.00).
  • Net Nominal Return = 8.0% − 0.5% = 7.50%; Exact Fisher Net Real Return = (1.075 ÷ 1.025) − 1 = 4.8780%/yr.
  • Solving logarithmic compounding with $30,000/yr savings: t = ln[(1,375,000×0.04878 + 30,000) ÷ (120,000×0.04878 + 30,000)] ÷ ln(1.04878) = 20.91 years (Projected FIRE Age 52.9).
  • Coast FIRE Number today for Age 65 (33 yrs) = $1,375,000 ÷ (1.04878)^33 = $285,374.16.

Result: $1,375,000.00 FIRE Target (20.91 Years · Reached at Age 52.9)

Investing $2,500/month bridges the gap from $120,000 to full financial independence in under 21 years while preserving full inflation-adjusted purchasing power.

Regional Standards & Units

Retiring earlier than statutory pension ages requires bridging taxable/accessible wealth across local account lock-in rules:

  • United States: Bridge pre-59½ spending using Roth conversion ladders, Rule of 55, IRS 72(t) SEPP, or taxable brokerage accounts. (USD ($)) [2026]
  • United Kingdom: ISAs fund early years prior to NMPA pension access (age 55, rising to 57 in April 2028) and SPA 67. (GBP (£)) [2026–27]
  • Canada: TFSA + non-registered investments bridge before CPP/OAS or RRIF withdrawals. (CAD (C$)) [2026]
  • Australia: Outside-super ETFs bridge the gap until Super preservation age 60. (AUD (A$)) [FY 2026–27]

Applying 4.0% SWR to a 50-Year Horizon Without Healthcare or Tax Buffers

Forgetting post-employment health insurance ($6k–$15k/yr) or ignoring early-retirement market crash sensitivity (sequence-of-returns risk) can cause a tight 4.0% budget to fail in bad market decades.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 4% Rule and is it safe for early retirees?

Derived from the Bengen and Trinity studies over 30-year rolling retirements, 4% (25× annual spending) assumes historic balanced stock/bond portfolios. Early retirees facing 40–50 year horizons frequently stress-test 3.25%–3.50% SWR (~28.6×–30.8× expenses).

Why does this calculator convert returns via the Fisher Equation?

Computing exact net real return (1 + r_nom − drag)/(1 + inflation) − 1 keeps your target FIRE Number, Lean/Fat FIRE bands, and Coast FIRE milestones anchored in today’s familiar purchasing power.

What is Coast FIRE?

Coast FIRE is the portfolio value needed today such that, without adding another dollar of future savings, real compound growth alone reaches your full FIRE target by traditional retirement age (such as 65).
