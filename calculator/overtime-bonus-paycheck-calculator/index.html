How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Overtime & Bonus Paycheck Calculator separates Regular Pay, Time-and-a-Half Overtime (1.5×), Double-Time (2.0×), and Cash Bonuses—applying IRS Publication 15’s statutory 22% flat supplemental withholding rate (37% above $1M) alongside regular tax withholding, 7.65% FICA, and state payroll taxes.

Employees often think bonuses are "taxed at a higher rate" because employers withhold a mandatory 22% federal flat rate on supplemental cash bonuses (per IRS Publication 15) rather than your lower effective W-4 rate. Remember: withholding is only a prepayment—any excess withheld on a bonus is refunded when you file your annual return.

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Biweekly paycheck at $36/hr for 80 regular hours + 12 overtime hours (1.5×) + $1,500 cash bonus (15% regular Fed rate, 4.5% state rate).

  • Regular Pay = 80 × $36 = $2,880.00; Overtime Pay = 12 × $54 = $648.00; Bonus = $1,500.00 → Total Gross = $5,028.00.
  • Federal Withholding: Regular Wages ($3,528 × 15%) = $529.20; Bonus (IRS 22% flat) = $330.00.
  • FICA (7.65% × $5,028) = $384.64; State Tax (4.5% × $5,028) = $226.26 → Total Withheld = $1,470.10.

Result: $3,557.90 Net Take-Home Paycheck (Out of $5,028.00 Gross)

Even though the $1,500 bonus has $330 (22%) withheld for federal taxes upfront, your true tax owed depends on annual total taxable income at filing.

Assuming the 22% Bonus Withholding Rate Is Your Final Tax Rate

Your bonus is taxed identically to regular salary on Form 1040. If your true marginal tax bracket is 12%, you receive the extra 10% back as a tax refund.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my paycheck show 22% federal tax withheld on a bonus even if my tax bracket is 12% or 24%?

Under IRS Publication 15 (Circular E), employers paying supplemental wages separately use the optional 22% flat federal withholding rate up to $1 million (and 37% above $1M). Any over- or under-withholding reconciles accurately when you file Form 1040.

How are US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) overtime and double-time calculated?

Non-exempt workers receive 1.5× their regular hourly rate for overtime hours (generally above 40 hours/week federally, or >8 hours/day in jurisdictions like California), and 2.0× regular rate for qualifying double-time holiday or extended shifts.

Are overtime and bonuses still subject to 7.65% FICA payroll taxes?

Yes. Regular wages, overtime, and cash bonuses are all fully subject to 6.2% Social Security (until cumulative YTD wages pass $184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% Medicare taxes.
