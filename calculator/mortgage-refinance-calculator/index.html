How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Mortgage Refinance Calculator evaluates whether refinancing an existing mortgage lowers monthly payments and increases net wealth after accounting for upfront or rolled-in closing costs, remaining amortization term reset, and your expected years in the home.

Lowering your interest rate by 1.0%–1.5% does not automatically save money if you reset a 25-year remaining loan back to 30 years or sell the home before recouping $6,400+ in closing fees. This tool separates simple monthly payment relief from break-even month recovery and horizon-adjusted net wealth (cash saved plus remaining principal reduction difference minus closing costs).

Governing Equation
Break-Even = Closing Costs ÷ (PMT_old − PMT_new) | NetBenefit_H = ΔPMT·H + (Bal_old,H − Bal_new,H) − Costs

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Refinancing a $320,000 remaining mortgage (7.125%, 324 months / 27 yrs left) into a new 30-year fixed loan at 5.75% with $6,400 closing costs paid upfront, planning to stay 7 years.

  • Current Monthly P&I ($320k @ 7.125% over 324 mos) = $2,227.15/mo (remaining interest = $401,596.60).
  • New Refinanced Monthly P&I ($320k @ 5.75% over 360 mos) = $1,867.43/mo → Monthly Savings = $359.72/mo.
  • Simple Break-Even = $6,400 ÷ $359.72 = 17.8 months (~18 months / 1.5 yrs).
  • At Year 7, net wealth advantage combining cash-flow savings, principal balance difference, and $6,400 closing costs = +$22,772.32 (lifetime cash savings = $42,920.40).

Result: $359.72/mo Savings | 18-Month Break-Even | +$22,772.32 Net 7-Year Benefit

Because the 18-month break-even sits well inside the 7-year stay, refinancing builds $22,772+ of net wealth even after factoring in closing costs and the 3-year term extension.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is the mortgage refinance break-even point calculated?

Simple break-even divides upfront refinance closing costs and points by the monthly P&I payment savings (Closing Costs ÷ Monthly Savings). Our engine also calculates your Net Wealth Benefit over your expected years in the home by factoring in remaining principal balance differences at sale.

Does rolling closing costs into the new loan eliminate the break-even requirement?

No. Rolling closing costs into the mortgage increases your new financed principal, which slightly lowers monthly cash savings and increases lifetime interest accrued on those rolled fees. Always compare your expected hold horizon against the break-even month.

Why does resetting to a fresh 30-year mortgage sometimes increase total lifetime interest?

If you already paid down 5–10 years of your existing loan, stretching the remaining balance back out to 360 months adds extra years of interest compounding even at a lower rate. Testing a 15-year or 20-year refinance term—or paying extra principal—avoids resetting the amortization clock.
