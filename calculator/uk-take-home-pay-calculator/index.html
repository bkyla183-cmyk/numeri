How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The UK Take-Home Pay Calculator (2026–27) translates gross annual salary into exact annual, monthly, and weekly net pay across England/Wales/NI and Scotland—incorporating HMRC 2026–27 PAYE bands, the £100k–£125,140 Personal Allowance taper, Class 1 Employee National Insurance (8%/2%), pension salary sacrifice, and Student Loan Plans 1–5 & Postgraduate.

Crossing £100,000 in the UK triggers an effective 60% marginal Income Tax band between £100,000 and £125,140 because every £2 of income strips £1 of your £12,570 tax-free Personal Allowance. Pension salary sacrifice reduces Adjusted Net Income below £100k while saving both 40%–60% tax and 2%–8% National Insurance simultaneously.

PA = max(0, 12,570 − max(0, AdjInc − 100,000)÷2) | NI = 8%(£12,570–£50,270) + 2%(>£50,270)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A £55,000 employee in England with 5% pension salary sacrifice and a Plan 2 Student Loan in tax year 2026–27.

  • Salary-Sacrificed Income = £55,000 − £2,750 = £52,250 (full £12,570 PA retained).
  • 2026–27 Income Tax: 20% on £37,700 (£7,540) + 40% on £1,980 above £50,270 (£792) = £8,332.00/yr.
  • Class 1 Employee NI: 8% on (£50,270 − £12,570) = £3,016 + 2% on (£52,250 − £50,270) = £39.60 → £3,055.60/yr.
  • Plan 2 Student Loan: 9% × (£52,250 − £29,385) = £2,057.85/yr → Net Pay = £38,804.55/yr (£3,233.71/mo).

Result: £3,233.71/month Net UK Take-Home Pay (£38,804.55/yr)

Notice how 5% pension sacrifice drops taxable earnings from £55,000 toward £52,250, avoiding the 40% rate on most of the pension contribution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is the UK effective marginal tax rate 60% between £100,000 and £125,140?

For every £2 of Adjusted Net Income above £100,000, HMRC withdraws £1 of the £12,570 Personal Allowance until it reaches £0 at £125,140. Combined with the 40% Higher Rate (or 42%–45% in Scotland) plus 2% Class 1 NI, earnings in this band suffer a 62%+ marginal deduction—making pension salary sacrifice highly effective.

What are the 2026–27 UK Student Loan thresholds modeled here?

For 2026–27, repayments are 9% above £29,385 (Plan 2), £26,065 (Plan 1), £32,745 (Plan 4 Scotland), and £25,000 (Plan 5), plus 6% above £21,000 for Postgraduate Loans. Salary-sacrifice pension contributions reduce NI-able earnings on which loan deductions are calculated.

How do 2026–27 Scottish Income Tax rates differ from England, Wales, and Northern Ireland?

Scotland applies a 6-band schedule (19% Starter, 20% Basic, 21% Intermediate, 42% Higher, 45% Advanced, and 48% Top), whereas England, Wales, and Northern Ireland use 3 main bands above the Personal Allowance (20%, 40%, and 45%).
