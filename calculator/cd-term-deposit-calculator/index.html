How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The CD & Term Deposit Calculator compares Compound Interest Certificates of Deposit (where interest reinvests inside the certificate) against Simple Interest Term Deposits (where interest pays out periodically without compounding), reporting APY/AER, gross maturity balance, and net after-tax maturity value.

While most retail US Certificates of Deposit compound interest daily or monthly and roll it into the balance, many term deposits in Canada (non-compounding GICs), Australia, and the UK allow simple-interest monthly or annual coupon payouts. Measuring the exact dollar gap between Compound vs. Simple interest—and applying your marginal income tax rate—shows your true net yield before locking up cash.

Governing Equation
Compound: FV = P(1 + r/m)^(mt), APY = (1 + r/m)^m − 1 | Simple: FV = P(1 + r·t)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Depositing $25,000 into a 24-month CD at 4.80% nominal APR compounded monthly, subject to a 22% marginal income tax rate.

  • Effective APY/AER = (1 + 0.048 ÷ 12)^12 − 1 = 4.9070%.
  • Compound Maturity Balance = $25,000 × (1.004)^24 = $27,513.71 ($2,513.71 gross interest).
  • Simple Interest Benchmark (if interest paid out without reinvesting) = $25,000 × (1 + 0.048 × 2) = $27,400.00 (difference: +$113.71).
  • After 22% tax on earned interest ($553.02), net after-tax interest = $1,960.69 → After-Tax Maturity = $26,960.69.

Result: $27,513.71 Gross Maturity ($26,960.69 After-Tax · 4.9070% APY)

Monthly compounding generates $113.71 more than a simple-interest payout certificate on the same 4.80% headline rate.

Regional Standards & Units

Fixed-rate deposit mechanics differ slightly in payout quoting and tax sheltering across markets:

  • United States: CDs quote APY (Reg DD); taxable interest reported annually on Form 1099-INT unless held inside an IRA CD. (USD ($)) [2026]
  • United Kingdom: Fixed-Rate Bonds quote AER; outside Cash ISAs, Personal Savings Allowance (£1,000 basic / £500 higher) shields partial interest. (GBP (£)) [2026–27]
  • Canada: Multi-year GICs frequently compound annually; annual accrued interest is taxable outside TFSA/RRSP/FHSA. (CAD (C$)) [2026]
  • Australia: Term Deposits frequently pay simple interest at maturity or monthly/quarterly intervals if selected. (AUD (A$)) [2026]

Ignoring Simple vs. Compound Payout Rules on Multi-Year Term Deposits

Selecting monthly interest payout to checking converts a 5-year compound term deposit into a simple-interest instrument, sacrificing exponential interest-on-interest growth.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Compound Interest CDs and Simple Interest Term Deposits?

Compound CDs keep earned interest inside the certificate where it earns interest-on-interest up to APY/AER; simple-interest deposits pay coupon interest out to checking periodically so principal stays flat.

When is CD or Term Deposit interest actually taxed?

Outside tax shelters (IRA, Cash ISA, TFSA), multi-year bank CDs in the US and GICs in Canada typically trigger annual income tax on interest accrued or credited each calendar year via 1099-INT or T5—not all in the maturity year.

What does APY or AER mean versus stated nominal rate?

Stated nominal APR is the annualized coupon rate before intra-year compounding; APY (US) and AER (UK) reflect your true 12-month compound yield (e.g., 4.80% APR compounded monthly = 4.907% APY/AER).
