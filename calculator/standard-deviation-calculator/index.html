How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A standard deviation calculator quantifies dataset spread around the arithmetic mean, computing both sample standard deviation (s, using N − 1 Bessel’s correction) and population standard deviation (σ, using N), along with variance, sum, median, and per-item squared deviations.

Mean alone hides volatility: two portfolios or classrooms can average 80 while one clusters tightly around 78–82 and the other swings from 40 to 120. Showing every item deviation (x − μ) and squared deviation makes statistical dispersion intuitive.

Governing Equation
s = √[ Σ(xᵢ - x̄)² / (N - 1) ] | σ = √[ Σ(xᵢ - μ)² / N ]

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Evaluating Sample Standard Deviation for dataset [10, 12, 23, 23, 16, 23, 21, 16].

  • Compute mean x̄ = (10 + 12 + 23 + 23 + 16 + 23 + 21 + 16) / 8 = 144 / 8 = 18.0.
  • Sum squared deviations Σ(x - 18)² = 64 + 36 + 25 + 25 + 4 + 25 + 9 + 4 = 192.
  • Divide by N - 1 = 7 to get sample variance: 192 / 7 = 27.4286.
  • Take square root: s = √27.4286 = 5.2372.

Result: s = 5.2372 (Mean = 18; Sample Variance = 27.4286; Population σ = 4.8990)

Typical observations fall roughly ±5.24 units around the average of 18.

Using Population Divisor N on Small Sample Data

Applying N instead of N − 1 on small experimental samples systematically underestimates true population variability.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use sample (N − 1) or population (N) standard deviation?

Use sample standard deviation (dividing by N − 1, Bessel’s correction) when your dataset is a subset drawn from a larger group. Use population standard deviation (dividing by N) only when you possess measurements for every member of the entire group.

What is the relationship between variance and standard deviation?

Standard deviation is simply the positive square root of variance. Because variance is in squared units (like dollars² or inches²), taking the square root brings standard deviation back into the same unit as your original data.

Can standard deviation ever be negative?

No. Because squared deviations from the mean are always non-negative, standard deviation is always ≥ 0—and equals 0 only when every data value is identical.
