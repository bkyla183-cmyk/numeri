How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A percentage calculator solves the three core ratio problems of everyday math: finding a percentage of a number, determining what percentage one value is of another, and computing relative percentage increase or decrease.

Percentages express ratios standardized out of 100, making disparate scales immediately comparable across discounts, test grades, margins, and economic growth rates. Remembering which value serves as the denominator baseline keeps relative changes mathematically consistent.

Governing Equation
P% of Y = (P / 100) × Y | X as % of Y = (X / Y) × 100 | Change % = [(New - Old) / |Old|] × 100

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Finding what percentage 25 is of 200.

  • Divide part by whole: 25 ÷ 200 = 0.125.
  • Multiply decimal by 100: 0.125 × 100 = 12.5%.

Result: 12.5%

25 represents one-eighth (12.5%) of a total baseline of 200.

Adding Sequential Percentages Together Directly

Applying a 20% discount followed by another 20% off yields a 36% total discount (0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64), not 40%.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you find what percentage one number is of another?

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 25 out of 200 equals (25 ÷ 200) × 100 = 12.5%.

Why does a 20% decrease followed by a 20% increase not return to 100%?

Percentages always apply to the current baseline. Decreasing 100 by 20% yields 80, and increasing 80 by 20% adds only 16—landing at 96 rather than 100.

What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

An interest rate rising from 4.0% to 5.0% increases by 1.0 percentage point in absolute terms, which represents a 25% relative increase.
