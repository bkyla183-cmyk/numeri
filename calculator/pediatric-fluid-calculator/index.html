How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Pediatric Fluid Maintenance Calculator computes hourly and 24-hour intravenous (IV) maintenance rates using the gold-standard Holliday-Segar 4-2-1 (hourly) and 100-50-20 (daily) rules in kg or lb, with optional 5% or 10% dehydration deficit replacement.

Developed by Malcolm Holliday and William Segar in 1957, the 4-2-1 method scales fluid requirements tightly to pediatric metabolic expenditure: 4 mL/kg/hr for the first 10 kg, 2 mL/kg/hr for the second 10 kg (10–20 kg), and 1 mL/kg/hr for every kilogram above 20 kg. Note a classic clinical nuance: because 4×24 = 96, 2×24 = 48, and 1×24 = 24, multiplying the hourly 4-2-1 rate by 24 hours yields slightly less (~2.8%) than the rounded 100-50-20 mL/kg/day mnemonic—this tool transparently shows both.

Hourly (4-2-1) = 4·min(W,10) + 2·clamp(W−10,0,10) + 1·max(W−20,0) | Deficit = 10 × %Dehyd × W

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: A well-hydrated (0% deficit) 24 kg child requires standard inpatient baseline IV maintenance fluids.

  • First 10 kg: 10 × 4 = 40 mL/hr (10 × 100 = 1,000 mL/day).
  • Next 10 kg (11–20 kg): 10 × 2 = 20 mL/hr (10 × 50 = 500 mL/day).
  • Remaining 4 kg (>20 kg): 4 × 1 = 4 mL/hr (4 × 20 = 80 mL/day) → 64.0 mL/hr hourly rate (1,580 mL/day via 100-50-20 rule).

Result: 64.0 mL/hr Baseline Maintenance (1,580 mL/24h via 100-50-20 Rule)

Infusion pumps are set at 64.0 mL/hr; if 5% mild dehydration were present (+1,200 mL over 24h = +50.0 mL/hr), combined 24h infusion rate rises to 114.0 mL/hr.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does 64 mL/hr × 24 hours (1,536 mL) differ slightly from 1,580 mL/day on a 24 kg child?

This is a classic pediatric arithmetic distinction: Holliday-Segar’s daily 100-50-20 mL/kg rule rounds 4×24 = 96 up to 100, 2×24 = 48 up to 50, and 1×24 = 24 down to 20. Our calculator displays both the exact hourly 4-2-1 pump rate and the 100-50-20 daily total.

Can Holliday-Segar 4-2-1 be used for newborns under 28 days old?

No. Preterm and term neonates (<28 days old) follow specialized day-of-life total fluid allowances (starting ~60–80 mL/kg/day on Day 1) rather than standard Holliday-Segar maintenance.

What type of IV fluid does the AAP recommend with Holliday-Segar rates?

For pediatric patients aged 28 days to 18 years, the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends isotonic maintenance IV solutions (with appropriate potassium and dextrose) rather than hypotonic saline to prevent iatrogenic hyponatremia.
