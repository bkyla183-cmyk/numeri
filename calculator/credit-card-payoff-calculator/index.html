How It Works & Mathematical Principles

A credit card payoff calculator solves for the exact number of months and cumulative interest required to pay a revolving balance down to zero given an APR and a fixed monthly payment. It exposes how minimum payments trap borrowers in multi-year interest cycles.

Unlike fixed installment loans, credit card balances compound interest daily or monthly while minimum payments shrink slowly alongside the balance. Locking in a higher fixed monthly payment breaks that tail, ensuring every billing cycle eliminates progressively more principal until payoff.

Governing Equation
n = -ln(1 - r·P / PMT) / ln(1 + r)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Paying off a $6,500 credit card balance at 22.4% APR with a consistent $250 monthly payment.

  • Monthly interest rate r = 0.224 / 12 = 0.0186667 (Month 1 interest = $121.33).
  • Verify payment clears interest: $250 > $121.33, leaving $128.67 to cut principal in Month 1.
  • Evaluate logarithmic payoff horizon: n = -ln(1 - 121.333 / 250) / ln(1.0186667) = 35.87 -> 36 months.

Result: 36 months to payoff

Even at $250/month, paying down a 22.4% APR card takes 3 full years; bumping payments to $325/month cuts months and interest substantially.

Continuing New Purchases While Paying Down Old Debt

Adding new card charges resets the payoff clock and accrues daily interest immediately once the grace period is lost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why can paying only the minimum keep credit card debt alive for decades?

Credit card interest compounds monthly at high APRs (often 18%–29%). If your payment barely exceeds monthly accrued interest, almost nothing touches principal—and paying less than monthly interest causes negative amortization where the balance actually grows.

What happens when I enter a 0% promotional APR?

At 0% APR, 100% of every payment goes directly to principal, so payoff time equals your balance divided by your monthly payment rounded up to whole months.

What is the difference between the Debt Avalanche and Debt Snowball methods?

The Debt Avalanche prioritizes paying off the card with the highest APR first (minimizing total interest paid), while the Debt Snowball targets the smallest balance first to generate quick psychological wins.
