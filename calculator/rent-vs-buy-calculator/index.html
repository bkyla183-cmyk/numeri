How It Works & Mathematical Principles

The Rent vs Buy Calculator evaluates the true net wealth difference between purchasing a home versus renting a comparable property and investing the exact capital difference—both your upfront down payment/closing cash on Day 0 and every monthly cash-flow delta over your holding horizon.

Simple rent-versus-mortgage payment comparisons are deeply misleading because homeownership builds equity through amortization and appreciation while consuming unrecoverable cash via property tax, maintenance, insurance, and transaction fees. This symmetric simulation seeds a Renter Investment Portfolio with the buyer’s upfront Day-0 cash (down payment + buying closing costs) and deposits or withdraws the exact monthly outflow gap each month over the chosen holding horizon.

Governing Equation
Buyer Net Equity = HomeVal_Y(1 − Sell%) − RemLoan_Y vs. Renter Portfolio_Y(Day0 + Monthly Delta)

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Comparing buying a $450,000 home (20% down, 6.25% 30-yr mortgage, 3% buying costs, 6% selling costs, 3.5% appreciation, 1.2% prop tax, 1% maintenance, $1,800 ins) against renting for $2,400/mo (3% rent growth, $240 ins) and investing differences at 6.5%/yr over 7 years.

  • Day-0 Cash Seed = $90,000 down + $13,500 closing = $103,500.00 credited to Renter Portfolio; $360,000 loan P&I = $2,216.58/mo.
  • After 7 years of symmetric monthly cash flows, Projected Home Value = $572,525.67 and Remaining Loan = $324,120.93.
  • Buyer Net Sale Proceeds after 6% agent/exit fees ($34,351.54) and mortgage payoff = $214,053.20.
  • Renter Portfolio (investing Day-0 $103,500 plus/minus monthly cost deltas at 6.5%/yr) = $231,652.13 → Net Buy Advantage = -$17,598.93 (Renting + Investing Leads by $17,598.93).

Result: -$17,598.93 Net Buy Advantage at Year 7 (Renting + Investing Leads)

At a 6.25% mortgage rate and only a 7-year hold, 9% combined round-trip transaction fees plus property tax and maintenance slightly outweigh equity build-up. Extending the hold past ~9–10 years or refinancing lower typically flips the advantage toward buying.

Regional Standards & Units

Capital gains taxes on primary homes, mortgage deductibility, and Stamp Duty materially influence local break-even horizons:

  • United States: Primary home sale exclusion up to $250k/$500k (IRS Sec 121); itemized SALT & mortgage interest deductibility often eclipsed by standard deduction. (USD ($)) [2026]
  • United Kingdom: Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is upfront buyer cost; Principal Private Residence Relief exempts primary home gains. (GBP (£)) [2026–27]
  • Canada: Principal Residence Exemption avoids cap gains tax; provincial/municipal Land Transfer Taxes increase upfront buyer cash. (CAD (C$)) [2026]
  • Australia: Substantial State Stamp Duty (3%–5%) extends break-even horizon past 7+ years even with CGT main residence exemption. (AUD (A$)) [2026]

Comparing Mortgage P&I Alone Against Monthly Rent

Treating a $2,217 mortgage P&I payment as directly comparable to a $2,400 rent ignores property tax ($450+/mo), maintenance (~1%/yr), homeowners insurance, and lost return on the $103,500 cash down.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this calculator compare Day-0 down payment money fairly?

The simulation credits the exact cash a buyer locks up on Day 0 (down payment + buying closing costs) directly into the Renter Investment Portfolio at the start of Month 1, and invests or withdraws the exact monthly cash-flow delta each month.

Why can renting win at Year 7 even when monthly rent nearly matches mortgage P&I?

Buyers pay ~3% buying closing costs upfront, ~6% selling agent fees at exit, property taxes, homeowners insurance, and ~1%/yr maintenance—which often take 7 to 11 years of appreciation and loan paydown to overcome.

Which assumptions move the Buy vs. Rent break-even most?

Holding horizon, mortgage interest rate, local home appreciation vs. investment return spread, and transaction costs (such as Australian/UK Stamp Duty) dominate the final wealth difference.
