How It Works & Mathematical Principles

An auto loan calculator computes the monthly installment and total interest cost for financing a new or used vehicle after accounting for down payment, APR, and repayment term length in months.

Auto dealerships frequently negotiate around monthly payment targets by quietly extending loan terms to 72 or 84 months. Calculating the financed principal alongside total lifetime interest reveals whether a lower monthly quote actually inflates total vehicle cost.

Governing Equation
M = (Price - Down) · [r(1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]

Step-by-Step Worked Calculation Example

Scenario: Purchasing a $32,000 car with a $6,000 down payment ($26,000 financed) at 5.9% APR over 60 months.

  • Subtract down payment to find financed principal: $32,000 - $6,000 = $26,000.
  • Monthly periodic rate r = 0.059 / 12 = 0.00491667.
  • Compute monthly payment over 60 months: $501.44 per month.

Result: $501.44 / month ($30,086.40 total payments; $4,086.40 total interest)

A 20% upfront down payment keeps equity positive and avoids being upside-down during early depreciation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a trade-in reduce sales tax on a car purchase?

In many US states, sales tax is assessed only on the net difference between the vehicle price and your trade-in allowance, though several states tax the full purchase price—check your local DMV rules when budgeting.

What is a sensible auto loan term length?

Shorter 36- to 48-month terms minimize interest and help prevent owing more than the car is worth (negative equity) as the vehicle depreciates, whereas 72- to 84-month loans lower monthly payments at steep total interest cost.

How does down payment size change my monthly auto payment?

Every extra dollar put down up front reduces your financed loan principal dollar-for-dollar, lowering both monthly payments and cumulative interest paid over the loan term.
